Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter

Radiation such as light exhibits both wave nature and particle nature. Similarly, matter particles like electrons also exhibit wave properties.

Wave Nature of Light

Wave nature of light explains phenomena such as:

  • Interference
  • Diffraction
  • Polarisation

Wave velocity relation

\[ c = \nu \lambda \]

  • \(c\) = speed of light
  • \(\nu\) = frequency
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength
Photon Theory of Radiation

According to Planck’s quantum theory, radiation consists of packets of energy called photons.

\[ E = h\nu \]

Where

  • \(h\) = Planck constant
  • \(\nu\) = frequency

Momentum of photon

\[ p = \frac{h}{\lambda} \]

Photoelectric Effect

Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of suitable frequency falls on it.

\[ E_k = h\nu - \phi \]

  • \(E_k\) = kinetic energy of electron
  • \(\phi\) = work function

\[ E_k = eV_0 \]

de-Broglie Hypothesis

Louis de-Broglie proposed that matter particles also exhibit wave nature.

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} \]

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \]

  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength
  • \(p\) = momentum
de-Broglie Wavelength (Important Cases)
Electron accelerated through potential \(V\)

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} \]

In practical units

\[ \lambda = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}}\ \text{Å} \]

Davisson–Germer Experiment

Davisson and Germer experimentally verified the wave nature of electrons by observing diffraction of electrons from a nickel crystal.

This experiment confirmed de-Broglie’s hypothesis.

Phase Velocity and Group Velocity

Phase velocity

\[ v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} \]

Group velocity

\[ v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} \]

For matter waves, group velocity equals particle velocity.

Important Points for EAPCET
  • Light shows both wave and particle nature.
  • Matter particles also exhibit wave properties.
  • de-Broglie wavelength inversely proportional to momentum.
  • Davisson–Germer experiment proved electron diffraction.
