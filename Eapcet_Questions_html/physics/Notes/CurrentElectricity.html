⚡ Electric Current & Basic Concepts
Electric Current
Electric current: I = q/t SI unit: ampere (A) Dimension: [A] Equivalent current due to revolving charge: i = qf
  • Conventional current direction = direction of positive charge flow.
  • In metals, current is due to electrons; in electrolytes/ionized gases, due to ions + electrons.
  • For non-uniform conductor, current remains same through every cross-section.
Types of Current
TypeNatureSources
DC (Direct Current)Magnitude and direction do not change with timeCell, battery, DC dynamo
AC (Alternating Current)Magnitude changes continuously and direction reverses periodicallyAC dynamo, mains supply
AC is used in power transmission due to easy transformation and reduced loss at high voltage.
🧲 Drift Velocity, Relaxation Time & Current Density
Thermal velocity of free electrons ≈ 10⁵ m s⁻¹ Average thermal velocity in any direction = 0 Relaxation time: τ = mean free path / v_rms Drift velocity: v_d = eEτ/m = eVτ/(ml) where, e = electron charge, m = electron mass, E = electric field, V = potential difference, l = conductor length
Relation with current: I = n A e v_d ⇒ v_d = I/(nAe) Current density: J = I/A = n e v_d SI unit: A m⁻² Dimension: [A L⁻²] J = σE (microscopic Ohm’s law) Direction of J: same as conventional current
Mobility of Electrons
Mobility μ = v_d/E = eτ/m SI unit: m² V⁻¹ s⁻¹ Dimension: [M⁻¹ T² A]
Large mobility means electrons acquire high drift speed for a given electric field.
📏 Ohm’s Law, Resistance & Resistivity
Ohm's law (at constant physical conditions): V ∝ I ⇒ V = IR Resistance: R = V/I SI unit: ohm (Ω) Dimension: [M L² T⁻³ A⁻²] For wire: R = ρl/A
Ohmic conductors (metals) follow linear V-I graph. Non-ohmic: diode, transistor, vacuum tube etc.
Microscopic resistance relation: R = ml/(A n e² τ) Resistivity: ρ = m/(n e² τ) Conductivity: σ = 1/ρ = ne²τ/m Unit: Ω⁻¹ m⁻¹ = S m⁻¹ = mho m⁻¹ Relation: J = σE
Geometrical Variations of Resistance
If volume remains constant and wire is stretched: R ∝ l² ⇒ R₁/R₂ = (l₁/l₂)² Since A = πr², R ∝ 1/r⁴ ⇒ R₁/R₂ = (r₂/r₁)⁴
Temperature Dependence of Resistivity
For metals: ρ_t = ρ₀(1 + αt) where α = temperature coefficient of resistivity α > 0 : metals (ρ increases with T) α small positive : alloys (nichrome, manganin, constantan) α < 0 : semiconductors, insulators (ρ decreases with T)
  • Resistivity depends on nature of material and temperature.
  • Resistivity is independent of dimensions (length/area).
  • In magnetic field: resistivity of most metals increases; for ferromagnets (Fe, Ni, Co) it decreases.

🔗 Combination of Resistors
Series Combination
Equivalent resistance: R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ... Properties: 1) Current through each resistor is same. 2) Total potential difference = sum of potential drops.
Parallel Combination
1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ... Properties: 1) Potential difference across each resistor is same. 2) Source current = sum of branch currents.
If n identical resistors are connected: Series first ⇒ R_s = nR Parallel first ⇒ R_p = R/n Hence: R_s / R_p = n² For a cube of 12 equal resistors (each R): (a) Across body diagonal = 5R/6 (b) Across face diagonal = 3R/4 (c) Along an edge = 7R/12
🔋 Electric Cell, emf & Internal Resistance
TypeFeatureExamples
Primary cellCannot be rechargedVoltaic, Daniel, Leclanche
Secondary cellRechargeableAcid accumulator, alkali accumulator
emf of cell: ε = W/q (energy per unit charge for complete circuit) Unit: volt (V) Terminal potential difference V: Energy per unit charge delivered in external circuit V < ε (when cell is discharging)
Internal resistance r: Obstruction offered by electrolyte inside cell. Variation of r: 1) Increases with concentration of electrolyte 2) Increases with distance between electrodes 3) Decreases with area of electrode dipped Relations (discharging): ε = V + Ir r = (ε/V − 1)R Charging state: ε = V − Ir
🔋 Grouping of Cells
(i) Cells in Series
For n cells (each emf ε, internal resistance r): ε_eq = nε r_eq = nr Current: I = ε_eq/(R + r_eq) = nε/(R + nr)
(ii) Cells in Parallel
For n identical cells in parallel: ε_eq = ε r_eq = r/n Current: I = ε/(R + r/n)
(iii) Mixed Grouping
n cells in series in each row, m such rows parallel: ε_eq = nε r_eq = nr/m I = nε / (R + nr/m) = m n ε / (mR + nr) Maximum current condition: R = nr/m (i.e., external resistance = equivalent internal resistance)
🧮 Kirchhoff’s Laws & Bridge Circuits
Kirchhoff’s Laws
1) Junction rule: ΣI = 0 at a junction (Conservation of charge) 2) Loop rule: ΣΔV = 0 around any closed loop (Conservation of energy)
These two laws are used to solve multi-loop and multi-branch circuits where simple series/parallel reduction is not possible.
Wheatstone Bridge

Arrangement of four resistances, one unknown, with galvanometer in bridge arm.

Balance condition (I_G = 0): P/Q = R/S Unknown resistance: S = (Q/P)R
Bridge is most sensitive when all four resistances are of same order.
Meter Bridge (Slide Wire Bridge)
At balance point: R/S = l₁/(100 − l₁) where l₁ = balancing length from one end (in cm)
  • Simplest practical form of Wheatstone bridge.
  • Used for precise comparison of resistances and finding unknown resistance.
📐 Potentiometer
Potentiometer is an ideal voltmeter for accurate potential difference comparison and internal resistance measurement.
Principle & Potential Gradient
For uniform wire carrying constant current: V ∝ l ⇒ V = kl k = potential gradient Unit: V m⁻¹ Sensitivity increases by increasing potentiometer wire length.
Comparison of emf of Two Cells
If balancing lengths are l₁ and l₂: ε₁ = x l₁ I ε₂ = x l₂ I Hence: ε₁/ε₂ = l₁/l₂
Internal Resistance of a Cell using Potentiometer
Without load (K₂ open): ε = k l₁ With load resistance R (K₂ closed): V = k l₂ Internal resistance: r = ((ε − V)/V)R = ((l₁ − l₂)/l₂)R
