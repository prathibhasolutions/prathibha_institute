🔬 Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors
  • Intrinsic: pure semiconductor; electrons and holes are generated thermally.
  • Extrinsic: doped semiconductor to increase conductivity.
  • n-type: pentavalent impurity (donor), majority carriers are electrons.
  • p-type: trivalent impurity (acceptor), majority carriers are holes.
Mass action law: np = nᵢ² For intrinsic semiconductor: n = p = nᵢ
⚡ p-n Junction & Diode Characteristics

At p-n junction, a depletion region and potential barrier are formed.

Diode current equation: I = I₀ (e^{eV/(ηkT)} - 1) Dynamic resistance: r_d = ΔV/ΔI
BiasConnectionBehavior
Forward biasp to +, n to −Barrier decreases, large current after knee voltage
Reverse biasp to −, n to +Small reverse saturation current
Typical knee voltage: ~0.2 V (Ge), ~0.7 V (Si). Breakdown may be Zener or avalanche.
🧪 Special Diodes and Rectifiers
  • Zener diode: heavily doped reverse-biased diode used in breakdown region as voltage regulator.
  • LED: emits light in forward bias due to recombination.
  • Photodiode: reverse-biased diode for light detection.
  • Solar cell: converts light energy to electrical energy.
Rectification: Half-wave rectifier: ripple frequency = input frequency Full-wave rectifier: ripple frequency = 2 × input frequency
🔁 Transistors, Amplifiers & Oscillator

BJT types: p-n-p and n-p-n; terminals are emitter, base and collector.

Current gains: α = I_C/I_E (common base) β = I_C/I_B (common emitter) γ = I_E/I_B (common collector) Relations: β = α/(1-α), α = β/(1+β), γ = 1 + β
  • CE amplifier gives current, voltage and power gain.
  • Transistor as switch: cut-off (OFF), saturation (ON).
LC oscillator frequency: f = 1/(2π√(LC))
