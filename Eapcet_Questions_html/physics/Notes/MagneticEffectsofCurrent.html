Physics Handbook — Chapter 22

Magnetic Effects of Current

Biot-Savart Law · Ampere Law · Lorentz Force · Cyclotron · Moving Coil Galvanometer

🧭 Oersted Experiment & Magnetic Field Basics

Oersted showed that a current-carrying conductor produces magnetic field (compass needle deflects near wire).

Magnetic field unit: 1 tesla = 1 Wb m⁻² = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ 1 gauss = 10⁻⁴ tesla
Direction rules: Maxwell’s cork screw rule, Ampere’s swimming rule, Right-hand thumb rule.
📘 Biot-Savart Law & Standard Field Results
Biot-Savart law: dB = (μ₀/4π) (I dl sinθ / r²) Vector form: dB = (μ₀/4π) [I (dl × r̂)/r²]
ConfigurationMagnetic field
Infinite straight conductor at distance r\(B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\)
Centre of circular loop (radius R)\(B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}\); for N turns: \(B=\frac{\mu_0 NI}{2R}\)
Arc of angle θ (radian), radius R\(B=\frac{\mu_0 I\theta}{4\pi R}\)
Long solenoid (inside)\(B=\mu_0 nI\)
One end of long solenoid\(B=\frac{1}{2}\mu_0 nI\)
Toroid at radius r\(B=\frac{\mu_0 NI}{2\pi r}\)
🧲 Ampere Circuital Law
Ampere’s circuital law: ∮ B·dl = μ₀ I_enclosed

Valid for any closed path; very useful for high-symmetry current distributions (wire, solenoid, toroid, cylindrical conductors).

⚡ Force on Moving Charges and Currents
Magnetic force on moving charge: F = q(v × B), |F| = qvB sinθ Lorentz force: F = q(E + v × B)
For q moving ⟂ B: r = mv/(qB), ω = qB/m, T = 2πm/(qB) Force on current carrying conductor: F = BIl sinθ Force between parallel currents: F/l = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)
Parallel currents in same direction attract; opposite directions repel. Ampere definition is based on this force relation.
🌀 Cyclotron
Cyclotron frequency: ν = qB/(2πm) Radius of path: r = mv/(qB) Maximum kinetic energy: K_max = q²B²r₀²/(2m) Maximum speed: v_max = qBr₀/m

Cannot accelerate neutral particles (e.g., neutron). Relativistic mass increase limits speed.

🧪 Moving Coil Galvanometer, Ammeter, Voltmeter
Torque on coil: τ = NBIA Restoring torque = Cθ At equilibrium: NBIA = Cθ Current sensitivity: θ/I = NBA/C Voltage sensitivity: θ/V = (NBA)/(CG)
Ammeter conversion (shunt S): S = (Ig G)/(I - Ig) (I = ammeter range, Ig = full-scale galvanometer current) Voltmeter conversion (series resistance R): R = (V/Ig) - G
Ideal ammeter resistance = 0, ideal voltmeter resistance = ∞.
