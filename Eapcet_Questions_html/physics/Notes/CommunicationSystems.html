📡 Communication Basics

Communication is faithful transmission of information from one place to another.

BlockFunction
TransmitterProcesses and encodes information to make it suitable for transmission
Communication channelMedium through which information propagates from transmitter to receiver
ReceiverReceives and decodes the signal for user
A practical communication system chain is:
Information source → Transmitter → Channel (with noise) → Receiver → User.
Message signal: A time varying electrical signal generated by transducer from original signal. It is a single valued function of time carrying information.
🔊 Message Signals & Modulation
Types of Message Signals
TypeDefinition
Analog signalCurrent/voltage changes continuously with time
Digital signalCurrent/voltage has two discrete values
Analog signal can be converted to digital and vice-versa.
Modulation & Need
Modulation: Superimposing audio/message signal on high frequency carrier wave. One carrier parameter (amplitude/frequency/pulse) is varied according to instantaneous modulating signal.

Need for modulation:

  • Low frequency audio waves (20 Hz–20 kHz) carry very small energy.
  • Efficient radiation/reception would require impractically large antennas (~5000 m) without modulation.
  • Audio frequency range is small; direct transmission causes overlap/confusion.
Types of Modulation & Demodulation
TypeWhat changes in carrier?Key relation
Amplitude Modulation (AM)Amplitude varies as per message signalBandwidth required = 2 × modulating frequency
Frequency Modulation (FM)Frequency varies as per message signalUsed for better noise immunity/music channels
Pulse ModulationContinuous waveform sampled at regular intervalsInformation sent at sampling instants
AM modulation index: m = (Change in amplitude of carrier wave) / (Amplitude of carrier wave) Demodulation: Process of separating audio/message signal from modulated wave.

Earth Atmosphere & Ionospheric Layers
  • Troposphere: up to 12 km
  • Stratosphere: 12–50 km (ozone layer around 30–50 km)
  • Mesosphere: 50–80 km
  • Ionosphere: 80–400 km

Variation in signal strength due to interference is called fading.
🛰️ Microwave & Satellite Communication
Microwave propagation: • Used in radar to locate flying objects. • Can be transmitted as directional beam signals better than many radio waves. • Show negligible diffraction around obstacle corners.
Satellite Communication
  • Uses a communication satellite (microwave receiver + transmitter) as repeater.
  • Geostationary satellite (period = 24 h) appears fixed relative to earth.
  • Indian remote sensing satellites (as listed): IRS-IA, IRS-IB, IRS-IC.
MeritsDemerits
1) Wide coverage area
2) Effective mobile communication
3) Economical; cost almost independent of distance
4) Useful in remote/hilly areas
5) High data rate transmission
6) Accurate search/rescue/navigation 		1) Satellite hardware repair is nearly impossible in orbit
2) Time delay due to very long path length (>2×36000 km)
🧵 Optical Communication & Optical Fibre

Communication in optical frequency range (~1–1000 THz) is called optical communication.

Optical fibre structure: • Core (glass/plastic) of uniform refractive index • Cladding with refractive index less than core • Outer protective insulating jacket Types of optical fibre: 1) Single mode step index 2) Multi mode step index 3) Multi mode graded index
Applications of Optical Fibres
  1. Endoscopy: bundle of fibres (light pipe) transmits image from inaccessible body parts.
  2. Signal transmission: electrical signal converted to light and back.
  3. Telecom cables: one fibre can carry up to ~2000 telephone messages with low intensity loss.

🌐 Modern Communication Tools
Internet, Modem & Fax
Modem = Modulator + Demodulator Used to connect computers via telephone lines by modulating and demodulating signals.
  • Internet: large global network enabling high-speed exchange of information.
  • Applications: surfing, e-mail, file transfer, e-banking, e-commerce, e-booking, social networking.
  • Fax: electronic reproduction of document at distant place.
Mobile Telephony & GPS
  • Mobile phone sends/receives RF signals wirelessly.
  • Service area is divided into cells controlled by MTSO (Mobile Telephone Switching Office).
  • Typical operating frequency: UHF (~800–950 MHz).
GPS is a space-based satellite navigation system providing accurate time and location globally using a constellation of 24 satellites.
GPS facts from chapter: • Satellite altitude ≈ 20,000 km • Each satellite orbits earth twice per day • At least four satellites view any point on earth for accurate position fixing
