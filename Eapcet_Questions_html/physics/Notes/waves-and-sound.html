Waves and Sound

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one point to another without transfer of matter.

Sound is a type of mechanical longitudinal wave that requires a material medium for propagation.

Types of Waves
  • Mechanical Waves – Need medium (sound waves)
  • Electromagnetic Waves – Do not require medium (light waves)
  • Transverse Waves – Particle motion perpendicular to wave direction
  • Longitudinal Waves – Particle motion parallel to wave direction
Important Terms in Waves
  • Wavelength (λ) – Distance between two successive compressions or crests
  • Frequency (f) – Number of oscillations per second
  • Time Period (T) – Time for one oscillation
  • Amplitude (A) – Maximum displacement
  • Wave velocity (v) – Speed of propagation

\[ v = f\lambda \]

Velocity of Sound

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}} \]

  • \(B\) = Bulk modulus
  • \(\rho\) = Density of medium
Plane Progressive Simple Harmonic Wave

Equation of progressive wave

\[ y = a \sin 2\pi\left(\frac{t}{T} - \frac{x}{\lambda}\right) \]

or

\[ y = a \sin\left(\omega t - kx\right) \]

Where

  • \(y\) = displacement
  • \(a\) = amplitude
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength
  • \(T\) = time period
  • \(x\) = distance from origin
  • \(v\) = wave velocity
Important Relations for Progressive Wave

\[ v = \frac{\omega}{k} \]

Angular wave number

\[ k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]

Wavelength

\[ \lambda = \frac{2\pi}{k} \]

Time Period

\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \]

Vibrations in a Stretched String

When a string of length \(l\) is fixed at both ends and plucked, stationary transverse waves are produced.

If \(n\) is frequency of vibration

  • \[ n \propto \frac{1}{l} \]
  • \[ n \propto \sqrt{T} \]
  • \[ n \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}} \]

Combined relation

\[ n = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]

\(T\) = tension in string \(\mu\) = mass per unit length

Beats

Beats are periodic variations in sound intensity produced when two sound waves of slightly different frequencies interfere.

\[ f_{beats} = |f_1 - f_2| \]

Standing Waves
  • Nodes – zero displacement
  • Antinodes – maximum displacement
Vibrations in Open Organ Pipe

An open organ pipe is a pipe in which both ends are open. At both ends, antinodes are formed.

Length of pipe = L

Fundamental Mode (First Harmonic)

\[ L = \frac{\lambda}{2} \]

\[ f_1 = \frac{v}{2L} \]

First Overtone (Second Harmonic)

\[ L = \lambda \]

\[ f_2 = \frac{v}{L} = 2f_1 \]

Second Overtone (Third Harmonic)

\[ L = \frac{3\lambda}{2} \]

\[ f_3 = \frac{3v}{2L} = 3f_1 \]

General Formula

\[ f_n = \frac{nv}{2L} \]

Where

  • \(n = 1,2,3,4...\)
  • \(v\) = velocity of sound
  • \(L\) = length of pipe

Important Points for EAPCET
  • All harmonics are present in an open pipe.
  • Frequency ratio = \(1 : 2 : 3 : 4 ...\)
  • Both ends are antinodes.
  • Distance between two consecutive nodes or antinodes = \( \lambda/2 \)
Vibrations in Closed Organ Pipe

In a closed organ pipe, one end is closed and the other end is open.

Closed end → Node Open end → Antinode

Fundamental Mode

\[ L = \frac{\lambda}{4} \]

\[ f_1 = \frac{v}{4L} \]

First Overtone (Third Harmonic)

\[ L = \frac{3\lambda}{4} \]

\[ f_3 = \frac{3v}{4L} \]

Second Overtone (Fifth Harmonic)

\[ L = \frac{5\lambda}{4} \]

\[ f_5 = \frac{5v}{4L} \]

General Formula

\[ f_n = \frac{(2n-1)v}{4L} \]

Where \(n = 1,2,3...\)

Important Points for EAPCET
  • Only odd harmonics are present.
  • Frequency ratio = \(1 : 3 : 5 : 7 ...\)
  • Closed end → Node
  • Open end → Antinode
Doppler Effect

The apparent change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.

\[ f' = f \left(\frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}\right) \]
