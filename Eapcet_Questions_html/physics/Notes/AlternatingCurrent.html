~ AC Fundamentals
Alternating current: I = I₀ sin(ωt) where, I₀ = peak (maximum) current ω = angular frequency = 2πf = 2π/T f = frequency, T = time period
  • AC changes both magnitude and direction periodically.
  • Instantaneous value changes sinusoidally with time.
QuantityExpression
Angular frequency\(\omega = 2\pi f = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}\)
Time period\(T = \dfrac{1}{f}\)
Peak current\(I_0\)
Peak voltage\(V_0\)
In a complete cycle of ideal sinusoidal AC, average current is zero due to equal positive and negative halves.
📊 Mean, RMS, Form Factor & Peak Factor
Mean value over half cycle: I_mean = (2/π) I₀ = 0.637 I₀ V_mean = (2/π) V₀ = 0.637 V₀ Mean value over complete cycle: I_mean = 0
RMS values: I_rms = I₀/√2 = 0.707 I₀ V_rms = V₀/√2 = 0.707 V₀ Form factor: = I_rms / I_mean(half cycle) = 0.707/0.637 ≈ 1.11 Peak factor (crest factor): = I₀ / I_rms = √2 ≈ 1.414
🧲 Reactance, Impedance & Power Factor
QuantityFormulaTrend
Inductive reactance \(X_L\)\(X_L = \omega L = 2\pi fL = \dfrac{2\pi L}{T}\)\(X_L \propto f\)
Capacitive reactance \(X_C\)\(X_C = \dfrac{1}{\omega C} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi fC} = \dfrac{T}{2\pi C}\)\(X_C \propto 1/f\)
For DC (\(f=0\)): \(X_L=0\) and \(X_C=\infty\).
Impedance of LCR circuit: Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²] Power factor: cosφ = R/Z Average AC power: P_av = V_rms I_rms cosφ = VI cosφ (if V, I represent rms values)
\(\cos\phi\) determines usable power in AC circuits. Higher power factor means better efficiency.
🔁 AC Circuits (R, L, C, RC, LC, LCR)
(i) Pure Resistive Circuit (R)
E = E₀ sin(ωt) I = I₀ sin(ωt) Phase relation: current and voltage in same phase (φ = 0) Power factor: cosφ = 1 Average power: maximum (non-zero)
(ii) Pure Inductive Circuit (L)
E = E₀ sin(ωt) I = I₀ sin(ωt − π/2) Current lags voltage by π/2 X_L = ωL Power factor: cos 90° = 0 Average power: 0
(iii) Pure Capacitive Circuit (C)
E = E₀ sin(ωt) I = I₀ sin(ωt + π/2) Current leads voltage by π/2 X_C = 1/(ωC) Power factor: cos 90° = 0 Average power: 0
(iv) R-C Circuit
I = I₀ sin(ωt + φ) Current leads voltage by φ Impedance: Z = √[R² + (1/ωC)²] Phase angle: tanφ = (1/ωCR) Voltage relation: V² = V_R² + V_C²
(v) L-C Circuit
I = I₀ sin(ωt − φ) For ideal LC: Z ~ |X_L − X_C| If X_L > X_C, φ = +π/2 If X_L < X_C, φ = −π/2 If X_L = X_C at ω = 1/√(LC), then Z = 0 (ideal)
(vi) L-C-R Circuit (General AC Circuit)
E = E₀ sin(ωt) I = I₀ sin(ωt ± φ) Resultant voltage: V = √[V_R² + (V_L − V_C)²] Impedance: Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²] Power factor: cosφ = R/Z Average power: P_av = V_rms I_rms cosφ
🎯 Resonance, Q-Factor & Wattless Current
Series Resonance (RLC Series)
Resonance condition: X_L = X_C Resonant frequency: f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)) At resonance: Z_min = R Current = maximum Q-factor (sharpness): Q = (1/R)√(L/C)
Series resonant circuit is also called acceptor circuit.
Parallel Resonance
Resonance condition: X_L = X_C At resonance: Impedance = maximum Current = minimum
Wattless current: current that consumes no average power (purely reactive circuit, \(R=0\), \(\cos\phi=0\), \(P_{av}=0\)).
⏱️ LC Oscillations, Choke Coil & Transient Current
LC oscillation equation: d²q/dt² + (1/LC)q = 0 Angular frequency: ω = 1/√(LC) Frequency: ν = 1/(2π√(LC))
When a charged capacitor discharges through an ideal inductor, electrical oscillations of constant amplitude are produced.
Choke coil: A high inductance, negligible resistance coil used in AC circuits to control current with minimum power loss. Based on wattless current principle. Used in fluorescent tube circuits. Transient current: Current that changes for finite short duration while rising/decaying.
Growth/Decay in L-R and Charging/Discharging in R-C
ProcessExpressionTime Constant
Current growth in LR\(I = I_0(1 - e^{-Rt/L})\)\(\tau = L/R\), current reaches 63.2% of \(I_0\)
Current decay in LR\(I = I_0 e^{-Rt/L}\)\(\tau = L/R\), current falls to 36.8% of \(I_0\)
Charge growth in RC\(q = q_0(1 - e^{-t/RC})\)\(\tau = RC\), charge reaches ~63.2%
Charge decay in RC\(q = q_0 e^{-t/RC}\)\(\tau = RC\), charge falls to ~36.8%
⚙️ AC Generator, DC Motor & Transformer
AC Generator (Dynamo)

Converts mechanical energy into alternating electrical energy (based on electromagnetic induction).

Induced emf: e = NBAω sin(ωt) = e₀ sin(ωt) Main parts: 1) Armature (rectangular coil) 2) Field magnets 3) Slip rings 4) Carbon/metal brushes
DC Motor

Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Torque on current-carrying coil: τ = NBIA sinθ Motor efficiency: η = Back emf / Applied emf = E/V
Transformer

Device based on mutual induction that changes AC voltage/current level.

TypeConditionEffect
Step-up transformer\(N_s > N_p\)\(E_s > E_p\), \(I_s < I_p\)
Step-down transformer\(N_s < N_p\)\(E_s < E_p\), \(I_s > I_p\)
Transformation ratio: K = N_s/N_p = E_s/E_p = I_p/I_s For step-up: K > 1 For step-down: K < 1
Transformer works only with AC, not DC.
