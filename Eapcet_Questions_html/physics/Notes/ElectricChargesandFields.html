⚡ Electric Charge & Basic Properties

Charge is the property by virtue of which bodies exert electrostatic force on each other.

ConceptKey point
Types of chargePositive and Negative
Interaction ruleLike charges repel, unlike charges attract
ConductorAllows charge flow (Cu, Ag, Al)
InsulatorDoes not allow charge flow (glass, rubber, plastic)
Charging by inductionCharging without contact by bringing charged body nearby
Basic properties of charge: 1) Additivity: q_total = q₁ + q₂ + ... + qₙ 2) Quantisation: Q = ±ne n = 1,2,3,... and e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C 3) Conservation: Charge is neither created nor destroyed; only transferred.
🧲 Coulomb’s Law & Superposition Principle
Coulomb’s law (magnitude): F = K |q₁q₂|/r² = (1/4πϵ₀) |q₁q₂|/r² In vacuum: 1/(4πϵ₀) ≈ 9×10⁹ N m² C⁻² ϵ₀ = 8.854×10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻² In medium: K = 1/(4πϵ) where ϵ = Kϵ₀ K = dielectric constant = ϵ/ϵ₀
Vector form: F₂₁ = (1/4πϵ₀) (q₁q₂/r²) r̂₂₁ F₁₂ = −F₂₁ Electrostatic force is central and conservative.
Superposition principle: Net force on charge = vector sum of forces due to all other charges. For q₁ due to q₂,q₃,...,qₙ: F₁ = F₁₂ + F₁₃ + ... + F₁ₙ

🧭 Electric Field Lines & Field of Ring

Electric field line: Imaginary curve whose tangent gives direction of \(\vec E\) at that point.

  • Start on positive and end on negative charge.
  • Never intersect each other.
  • Do not form closed loops.
  • Closer lines indicate stronger field.
Field on axis of uniformly charged ring: E_x = (1/4πϵ₀) (q x)/(x² + R²)^(3/2) Special cases: 1) At centre (x=0): E=0 2) For x >> R: E ≈ (1/4πϵ₀) q/x² 3) E is maximum at x = R/√2 E_max = (1/4πϵ₀) [2q/(3√3 R²)]
🔌 Electric Potential & Equipotential Surfaces
Potential: V = W/q Potential difference: ΔV = V_A − V_B = W_AB/q Potential due to point charge: V = (1/4πϵ₀) q/r Potential due to system: V = Σ (1/4πϵ₀)(qᵢ/rᵢ)
Potential gradient: dV/dr Relation with field: E = − dV/dr Equipotential surface facts: • Potential same at every point • Electric field is normal to surface • Point charge → spherical equipotential surfaces • Uniform field → planar equipotential surfaces • Line charge → cylindrical equipotential surfaces
🚀 Motion of Charged Particle in Electric Field
Force: F = qE Acceleration: a = qE/m If initial velocity = 0: v = at = qEt/m Using potential difference ΔV: v = √(2qΔV/m) Momentum: p = mv = √(2mqΔV) Kinetic energy: K = ½mv² = qΔV
🧪 Electric Dipole
Dipole moment: p = q(2a) Direction: from −q to +q SI unit: C·m
Field and Potential due to Dipole
LocationElectric fieldPotential
Axial line\(E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2pr}{(r^2-a^2)^2}\) (approx for \(r\gg a\): \(E\approx\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2p}{r^3}\))\(V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^2-a^2}\) (approx: \(V\approx\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^2}\))
Equatorial line\(E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{(r^2+a^2)^{3/2}}\) (approx: \(E\approx\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^3}\))\(V=0\)
General point at angle \(\theta\)\(E \propto \frac{\sqrt{1+3\cos^2\theta}}{r^3}\)\(V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p\cos\theta}{r^2}\) for \(r\gg a\)
Dipole in External Field
Torque in uniform field: τ = pE sinθ = |p × E| τ_max = pE (at θ = 90°) Work done rotating dipole from θ₁ to θ₂: W = pE(cosθ₁ − cosθ₂) Potential energy: U = −pE cosθ = −p · E
Parallel alignment with \(\vec E\): stable equilibrium. Anti-parallel: unstable equilibrium. In non-uniform field, dipole experiences both force and torque.
📤 Electric Flux & Gauss’ Theorem
Electric flux through area element: dΦ_E = E · dS For closed surface: Φ_E = ∮ E · dS Gauss theorem: ∮ E · dS = q_enclosed/ϵ₀

🛡️ Conductor, Dielectrics and Capacitors (Exam Add-ons)
Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium: 1) Electric field inside conductor = 0 2) Potential inside = potential at surface (constant) 3) Surface field ∝ surface charge density Electrostatic shielding: Protection from external electric field using closed hollow metallic enclosure. Lightning conductor: Pointed metallic rod to safely transfer cloud charge to earth.
Dielectric constant: K = E₀/E = 1 + χ_e Polarisation: P = χ_e E Capacitance: C = q/V Parallel plate capacitor: C = Kϵ₀A/d Air: C₀ = ϵ₀A/d
