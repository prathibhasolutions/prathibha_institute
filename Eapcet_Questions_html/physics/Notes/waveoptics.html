Wave Optics

Huygens Principle · Interference · YDSE · Diffraction · Polarisation

🌊 Wave Theory, Wavefront & Huygens Principle

Wave optics connects ray ideas with wave behavior of light. It explains interference, diffraction and polarisation.

Wavefront: Locus of points vibrating in same phase. Types: spherical, cylindrical, plane.
Huygens principle: every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets; new wavefront is their forward envelope.
✨ Interference of Light

When two coherent light waves superpose, redistribution of intensity produces bright and dark fringes.

Resultant intensity: I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂)cosϕ For equal intensities (I₁ = I₂ = I₀): I = 4I₀ cos²(ϕ/2) Maximum intensity: Imax = (√I₁ + √I₂)² Minimum intensity: Imin = (√I₁ - √I₂)² Visibility (contrast): V = (Imax - Imin)/(Imax + Imin)
Conditions: Constructive: path difference Δx = nλ, phase ϕ = 2nπ Destructive: Δx = (2n+1)λ/2, phase ϕ = (2n+1)π
🔬 Young’s Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)
Fringe width: β = λD/d where λ = wavelength, D = slit-screen distance, d = slit separation Position of nth bright fringe: yn(bright) = nβ Position of nth dark fringe: yn(dark) = (2n-1)β/2 Angular fringe width: θ = λ/d
Change madeEffect on fringe width β
Increase λβ increases
Increase Dβ increases
Increase dβ decreases
Immerse setup in medium (refractive index μ)β' = β/μ
Thin transparent slab (thickness t, refractive index μ) in one path: Path shift = (μ-1)t Fringe shift = x₀ = D(μ-1)t/d
🧭 Diffraction

Diffraction is bending/spreading of light near edges or apertures.

Single slit Fraunhofer diffraction minima: a sinθ = nλ, n = 1,2,3... For small angles: yn(min) = nλD/a Angular width of central maximum: 2λ/a Linear width of central maximum on screen: W = 2λD/a
Diffraction grating (grating element = d = a+b): d sinθ = nλ Maximum possible order: nmax = d/λ Resolving power of grating: R = λ/Δλ = nN (N = total illuminated slits)
🧪 Polarisation

Polarisation proves transverse nature of light.

Law of Malus: I = I₀ cos²θ Brewster’s law: μ = tan i_p where i_p = polarising angle At Brewster angle: i_p + r = 90°
  • Plane polarised light: vibrations confined to one plane.
  • Unpolarised light: vibrations in all planes perpendicular to propagation.
  • Polarisation by reflection, double refraction and scattering are important methods.
