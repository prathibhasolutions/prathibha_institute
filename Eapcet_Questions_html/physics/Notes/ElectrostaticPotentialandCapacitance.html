Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Potential · Equipotential Surface · Dipole Potential Energy · Dielectrics · Capacitors

🔋 Electric Potential & Potential Difference
Electric potential: V = W/q SI unit: volt (V = J/C) Dimension: [M L² T⁻³ A⁻¹] Potential difference: ΔV = V_A - V_B = W_AB/q
Potential due to point charge: V = (1/4πϵ₀)(q/r) Potential due to system of charges: V = Σ (1/4πϵ₀)(qᵢ/rᵢ)
📐 Potential Gradient & Equipotential Surfaces
Potential gradient: dV/dr Relation with electric field: E = -dV/dr
  • Equipotential surface has same potential at all points.
  • Electric field is always perpendicular (normal) to equipotential surface.
  • Point charge → spherical equipotential surfaces.
  • Uniform field → parallel plane equipotential surfaces.
  • Line charge → cylindrical equipotential surfaces.
🧲 Dipole: Potential, Work, Torque, Potential Energy
LocationElectric PotentialElectric Field (far field)
Axial line\(V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^2-a^2} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^2}\)\(E \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2p}{r^3}\)
Equatorial line\(V = 0\)\(E \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^3}\)
General angle \(\theta\)\(V \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p\cos\theta}{r^2}\)Depends on \(\sqrt{1+3\cos^2\theta}\)
Torque on dipole in uniform field: τ = pE sinθ = |p×E| τ_max = pE (at θ = 90°) Work done rotating dipole from θ₁ to θ₂: W = pE(cosθ₁ − cosθ₂) Potential energy: U = -pEcosθ = -p·E
🛡️ Conductor, Shielding & Dielectrics
  • Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, electric field is zero.
  • Potential inside conductor is constant and equal to surface potential.
  • Electrostatic shielding: enclosing region in closed conducting shell.
  • Lightning conductor protects buildings by safely carrying charge to earth.
Dielectric constant: K = E₀/E = 1 + χₑ Polarisation: P = χₑE For vacuum: χₑ = 0
📦 Capacitors and Capacitance
Capacitance: C = q/V SI unit: farad (F) 1 μF = 10⁻⁶ F, 1 pF = 10⁻¹² F
ConfigurationFormula
Isolated spherical conductorC = 4πϵ₀KR (for air K=1)
Concentric spherical capacitor (radii a,b)C = 4πϵ₀ab/(b-a)
Parallel plate capacitorC = Kϵ₀A/d (air: C₀ = ϵ₀A/d)
Partial dielectric slab (thickness t)C = ϵ₀A/(d - t + t/K)
Conducting slab inserted (thickness t)C = ϵ₀A/(d - t)
Multiple dielectric slabsC = ϵ₀A / (t₁/K₁ + t₂/K₂ + ...)
Force between capacitor platesF = Q²/(2ϵ₀A)
Induced charge due to dielectric: q' = q(1 - 1/K)
🔗 Capacitor Combinations & Energy
CombinationResultEqual quantity
Series1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...Charge same
ParallelC = C₁ + C₂ + ...Voltage same
Energy stored in capacitor: U = (1/2)qV = (1/2)CV² = q²/(2C) Standard form: U = (1/2)CV² Energy density: u = U/Volume = (1/2)ϵ₀E²
Charge redistribution (two conductors connected): Common potential: V = (q₁+q₂)/(C₁+C₂) = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂)/(C₁+C₂) Energy loss: ΔU = [C₁C₂(V₁-V₂)²]/[2(C₁+C₂)] (loss as heat in connecting wire)
🧪 Charged Drops & Van-de-Graaff Generator
If n identical charged drops coalesce: Total charge = nq New capacitance = n^(1/3)C New potential = n^(2/3)V New surface charge density = n^(1/3)σ New potential energy = n^(5/3)U
Van-de-Graaff generator produces very high potential (few MV), based on corona discharge and charge residing on outer surface of spherical shell.
