Electromagnetic Waves

Displacement Current · Maxwell’s Equations · EM Wave Properties · Poynting Vector · Spectrum

📘 Displacement Current & Maxwell’s Equations
Displacement current: I_D = ϵ₀ (dΦ_E/dt) where Φ_E is electric flux.
Maxwell’s equations: 1) ∮ E·dS = q/ϵ₀ (Gauss law in electrostatics) 2) ∮ B·dS = 0 (Gauss law in magnetostatics) 3) ∮ E·dl = -dΦ_B/dt (Faraday law) 4) ∮ B·dl = μ₀(I_C + I_D) = μ₀(I_C + ϵ₀ dΦ_E/dt) (Ampere-Maxwell law)
🌊 Electromagnetic Waves: Equation & Core Properties
Plane progressive EM wave: E = E₀ sin(ωt − x/c) B = B₀ sin(ωt − x/c) ω = 2πν
  • Produced by accelerated charge particles.
  • Transverse in nature.
  • Electric field, magnetic field, and direction of propagation are mutually perpendicular.
  • Speed in vacuum: c = 1/√(μ₀ϵ₀) = 3×10⁸ m/s.
Field amplitude relation: c = E₀/B₀
⚡ Energy Transport, Intensity & Momentum
Poynting vector: S = (1/μ₀)(E × B) SI unit: W m⁻² It gives rate of flow of EM energy per unit area.
Average electric energy density: U_E = (1/4)ϵ₀E₀² Average magnetic energy density: U_B = (1/4)(B₀²/μ₀) In EM wave: U_E = U_B
Intensity: I = c = (1/2)ϵ₀E₀²c Momentum of EM wave: p = U/c Radiation pressure: Absorbing surface: p = U/c Reflecting surface: p = 2U/c
🌈 Electromagnetic Spectrum & Uses

Electromagnetic spectrum is arrangement of EM radiations in order of wavelength or frequency.
