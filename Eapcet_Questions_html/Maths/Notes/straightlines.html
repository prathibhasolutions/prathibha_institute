Straight Line

Definition, Slope & Angle Between Lines

A straight line is the locus of all points which are collinear with two given points. The general equation of a line is \(ax + by + c = 0\).

  • Through a fixed point in a given direction, there is one and only one line.
  • Through a given point, there are infinitely many lines.
Slope (Gradient) of a Line

The slope \(m\) of a line is the trigonometric tangent of the angle \(\theta\) that the line makes with the positive direction of the X-axis (anti-clockwise):

\[ m = \tan\theta \]
  • Slope of a line parallel to X-axis: \(m = 0\)
  • Slope of a line parallel to Y-axis: \(m = \infty\)
  • Slope of a line equally inclined to axes: \(m = 1\) or \(m = -1\) (angles \(45°\) or \(135°\))
  • Slope of a line through \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\): \[ m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \]
Angle Between Two Lines

The angle \(\theta\) between two lines with slopes \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) is:

\[ \tan\theta = \left|\frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2}\right| \]
  • Lines are parallel iff \(m_1 = m_2\)
  • Lines are perpendicular iff \(m_1 m_2 = -1\)

Different Forms of the Equation of a Straight Line

Standard Lines
  • Line parallel to X-axis at distance \(b\): \(y = b\)
  • Line parallel to Y-axis at distance \(a\): \(x = a\)
  • X-axis: \(y = 0\)
  • Y-axis: \(x = 0\)
(i) Slope–Intercept Form

Line with slope \(m\) and Y-intercept \(c\):

\[ y = mx + c \]

If passing through origin: \(y = mx\)

(ii) One Point–Slope Form

Line through \((x_1, y_1)\) with slope \(m\):

\[ y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \]
(iii) Two Points Form

Line through \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\):

\[ \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \]

Equivalently, by determinant:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} x & y & 1 \\ x_1 & y_1 & 1 \\ x_2 & y_2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 \]
(iv) Intercept Form

Line cutting X-intercept \(a\) and Y-intercept \(b\):

\[ \frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} = 1 \]

General equation \(Ax + By + C = 0\) converts to intercept form as:

\[ \frac{x}{-C/A} + \frac{y}{-C/B} = 1 \]
(v) Normal Form

If the perpendicular from origin has length \(p\) and makes angle \(\alpha\) with the X-axis:

\[ x\cos\alpha + y\sin\alpha = p \]
(vi) Distance (Parametric) Form

Line through \((x_1, y_1)\) making angle \(\theta\) with positive X-axis:

\[ \frac{x - x_1}{\cos\theta} = \frac{y - y_1}{\sin\theta} = r \]

where \(r\) is the distance from \((x_1, y_1)\) to the point \((x, y)\). The coordinates of any point at distance \(r\) from \((x_1, y_1)\) are:

\[ (x_1 + r\cos\theta,\; y_1 + r\sin\theta) \]

\(r > 0\) if the point is on the right side of \((x_1, y_1)\); \(r < 0\) if on the left side.

Position of Points, Concurrency & Distance

Position of Point(s) Relative to a Line

For points \(P(x_1, y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2)\) relative to line \(ax + by + c = 0\):

  • Same side if \((ax_1+by_1+c)\) and \((ax_2+by_2+c)\) have the same sign.
  • Opposite sides if they have opposite signs.
  • \((x_1, y_1)\) lies on same side as origin if \((ax_1+by_1+c)\) and \(c\) have the same sign.
  • \((x_1, y_1)\) lies on opposite side to origin if \((ax_1+by_1+c)\) and \(c\) have opposite signs.
Condition of Concurrency

Three lines \(a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0\), \(a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0\), \(a_3x+b_3y+c_3=0\) are concurrent if:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \\ a_3 & b_3 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} = 0 \]

Equivalently: \(a_1(b_2c_3 - b_3c_2) - b_1(a_2c_3 - a_3c_2) + c_1(a_2b_3 - a_3b_2) = 0\)

Distance of a Point from a Line

Perpendicular distance of point \((x_1, y_1)\) from line \(Ax + By + C = 0\):

\[ d = \frac{|Ax_1 + By_1 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}} \]

Distance of origin from line \(Ax + By + C = 0\):

\[ d = \frac{|C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}} \]
Distance Between Two Parallel Lines

Distance between \(y = mx + c_1\) and \(y = mx + c_2\):

\[ d = \frac{|c_1 - c_2|}{\sqrt{1 + m^2}} \]

Distance between \(ax + by + c_1 = 0\) and \(ax + by + c_2 = 0\):

\[ d = \frac{|c_2 - c_1|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \]
Point of Intersection of Two Lines

For lines \(a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0\) and \(a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0\), the point of intersection is:

\[ \left(\frac{b_1c_2 - b_2c_1}{a_1b_2 - a_2b_1},\; \frac{c_1a_2 - c_2a_1}{a_1b_2 - a_2b_1}\right) \]

Parallel, Perpendicular Lines & Image of a Point

Lines Parallel and Perpendicular to a Given Line
  • Line parallel to \(ax + by + c = 0\): \(\;ax + by + \lambda = 0\)
  • Line perpendicular to \(ax + by + c = 0\): \(\;bx - ay + \lambda = 0\)

(\(\lambda\) is an arbitrary constant determined by additional conditions.)

Image of a Point with Respect to a Line

Image of \((x_1, y_1)\) in line \(ax + by + c = 0\) is \((x_2, y_2)\) where:

\[ \frac{x_2 - x_1}{a} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{b} = \frac{-2(ax_1 + by_1 + c)}{a^2 + b^2} \]

Special cases:

  • Image of \(P(x_1, y_1)\) in X-axis: \(Q(x_1, -y_1)\)
  • Image of \(P(x_1, y_1)\) in Y-axis: \(Q(-x_1, y_1)\)
  • Image of \(P(x_1, y_1)\) in line \(y = x\): \(Q(y_1, x_1)\)
  • Image of \(P(x_1, y_1)\) in origin: \(Q(-x_1, -y_1)\)
  • Image of \(P(x_1, y_1)\) in line \(y = x\tan\theta\): \[ x_2 = x_1\cos 2\theta + y_1\sin 2\theta, \quad y_2 = x_1\sin 2\theta - y_1\cos 2\theta \]

Equation of Angle Bisectors

The equation of the bisectors of the angles between lines \(a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0\) and \(a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0\) are:

\[ \frac{a_1x + b_1y + c_1}{\sqrt{a_1^2 + b_1^2}} = \pm\frac{a_2x + b_2y + c_2}{\sqrt{a_2^2 + b_2^2}} \]
Identifying Acute and Obtuse Bisectors

First make the constant terms positive in both equations, then compute \(a_1a_2 + b_1b_2\):

ConditionPositive sign givesNegative sign gives
\(a_1a_2 + b_1b_2 > 0\) Obtuse bisector Acute bisector
\(a_1a_2 + b_1b_2 < 0\) Acute bisector Obtuse bisector

Pair of Straight Lines

The general second-degree equation \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0\) represents a pair of straight lines if and only if:

\[ \Delta = abc + 2fgh - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 = 0 \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix} = 0 \]
Homogeneous Equation of Second Degree

The equation \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0\) always represents two straight lines through the origin.

If the two lines have slopes \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), then:

\[ m_1 + m_2 = -\frac{2h}{b} = -\frac{\text{coeff of }xy}{\text{coeff of }y^2}, \qquad m_1 m_2 = \frac{a}{b} = \frac{\text{coeff of }x^2}{\text{coeff of }y^2} \]

The lines are:

  • Real and distinct if \(h^2 > ab\)
  • Coincident if \(h^2 = ab\)
  • Imaginary if \(h^2 < ab\)
Angle Between the Pair of Lines

For \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0\), the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines is:

\[ \tan\theta = \frac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{|a + b|} \]
  • Lines are coincident if \(h^2 = ab\)
  • Lines are perpendicular if \(a + b = 0\)

Note: The angle between lines represented by \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) equals the angle between lines represented by \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2=0\).

Angle Bisectors & Perpendicular Pair

Bisectors of the pair \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0\):

\[ \frac{x^2 - y^2}{a - b} = \frac{xy}{h} \quad\Rightarrow\quad hx^2 - (a-b)xy - hy^2 = 0 \]

Pair of lines perpendicular to \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0\) and through origin:

\[ bx^2 - 2hxy + ay^2 = 0 \]

Bisectors of the general pair \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) at intersection point \((x_1, y_1)\):

\[ \frac{(x-x_1)^2 - (y-y_1)^2}{a - b} = \frac{(x-x_1)(y-y_1)}{h} \]
Point of Intersection of the Pair

For the general pair \(ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0\), the point of intersection is:

\[ \left(\frac{hf - bg}{ab - h^2},\; \frac{gh - af}{ab - h^2}\right) \]
Parallel Lines from General Second Degree

The equation \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) represents two parallel lines if \(h^2 = ab\) and \(g^2 - ac > 0\). The distance between them is:

\[ 2\sqrt{\frac{g^2 - ac}{a(a+b)}} \quad\text{or}\quad 2\sqrt{\frac{f^2 - bc}{b(a+b)}} \]
Lines Joining Origin to Intersection of a Curve and a Line

To find the pair of lines joining the origin to the points of intersection of the curve \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) and the line \(lx+my+n=0\), homogenise the curve using \(1 = -\dfrac{lx+my}{n}\):

\[ ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx\!\left(\frac{-lx-my}{n}\right) + 2fy\!\left(\frac{-lx-my}{n}\right) + c\!\left(\frac{lx+my}{-n}\right)^{\!2} = 0 \]

Family of Lines & Division of a Line Segment

Family of Lines Through Intersection of Two Lines

The equation of the family of lines passing through the intersection of \(a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0\) and \(a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0\) is:

\[ (a_1x + b_1y + c_1) + \lambda(a_2x + b_2y + c_2) = 0 \quad (\lambda \in \mathbb{R}) \]
Division of a Line Segment by a Line

The line \(ax+by+c=0\) divides the segment joining \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) in the ratio \(\lambda : 1\), where:

\[ \lambda = -\frac{ax_1 + by_1 + c}{ax_2 + by_2 + c} \]
  • \(\lambda > 0\) → divides internally
  • \(\lambda < 0\) → divides externally
Line Through a Point Making Angle \(\alpha\) with a Given Line

Lines through \((x_1, y_1)\) making angle \(\alpha\) with \(y = mx + c\):

\[ y - y_1 = \frac{m \pm \tan\alpha}{1 \mp m\tan\alpha}(x - x_1) \]

Important Results to Remember

  • A triangle is isosceles if any two of its medians are equal.
  • In an equilateral triangle, orthocentre, centroid, circumcentre and incentre all coincide.
  • The circumcentre of a right-angled triangle is the mid-point of the hypotenuse.
  • Orthocentre, centroid, circumcentre of a triangle are collinear (Euler's line). Centroid divides the segment joining orthocentre and circumcentre in ratio \(2 : 1\).
  • If \(D, E, F\) are midpoints of sides \(BC, CA, AB\) of \(\triangle ABC\), then centroid of \(\triangle ABC\) = centroid of \(\triangle DEF\).
  • Orthocentre of a right-angled \(\triangle ABC\) right-angled at \(A\) is \(A\) itself.
  • Area of triangle formed by lines \(y = m_1x+c_1\), \(y = m_2x+c_2\), \(y = m_3x+c_3\): \[\Delta = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum\frac{(c_1-c_2)^2}{m_1-m_2}\right|\]
  • Area of triangle formed by line \(ax+by+c=0\) with coordinate axes: \[\Delta = \frac{c^2}{2|ab|}\]
  • Foot of perpendicular \((h, k)\) from \((x_1, y_1)\) to line \(ax+by+c=0\): \[\frac{h-x_1}{a} = \frac{k-y_1}{b} = -\frac{ax_1+by_1+c}{a^2+b^2}\]
  • Area of rhombus formed by \(ax \pm by \pm c = 0\): \[\Delta = \frac{2c^2}{ab}\]
  • Area of parallelogram formed by lines \(a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0\), \(a_1x+b_1y+d_1=0\), \(a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0\), \(a_2x+b_2y+d_2=0\): \[\Delta = \frac{(c_1-d_1)(c_2-d_2)}{|a_1b_2 - a_2b_1|}\]
  • Foot of perpendicular from \((a,b)\) on \(x - y = 0\): \(\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+b}{2}, \frac{a+b}{2}\right)\)
  • Foot of perpendicular from \((a,b)\) on \(x + y = 0\): \(\displaystyle\left(\frac{a-b}{2}, \frac{b-a}{2}\right)\)
  • Image of line \(a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0\) about line \(ax+by+c=0\): \[(aa_1+bb_1)(ax+by+c) = 2(a^2+b^2)(a_1x+b_1y+c_1)\] ... wait, the correct formula from the PDF is: replace \(a_1x+b_1y+c_1\) by its image. The combined form is \((aa_1+bb_1)^2 = (a^2+b^2)(a_1^2+b_1^2)\).
  • Third vertex of equilateral triangle given two vertices \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\): \[\left(\frac{x_1+x_2 \pm \sqrt{3}(y_1-y_2)}{2},\; \frac{y_1+y_2 \mp \sqrt{3}(x_1-x_2)}{2}\right)\]
  • Area of polygon with vertices \((x_1,y_1), (x_2,y_2), \ldots, (x_n,y_n)\) (in order): \[\Delta = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\right|\] (indices mod \(n\))
  • Pair of lines through \((\alpha,\beta)\) perpendicular to \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2=0\): \[b(x-\alpha)^2 - 2h(x-\alpha)(y-\beta) + a(y-\beta)^2 = 0\]
