Quadratic Expressions

Introduction & Graph of a Quadratic Expression

An expression of the form \(ax^2 + bx + c\), where \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(a \ne 0\), is called a quadratic expression in \(x\). Let \(y = f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c\).

Rewriting by completing the square:

\[y = a\!\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{D}{4a}\]

Setting \(X = x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\) and \(Y = y + \dfrac{D}{4a}\), we get \(Y = aX^2\) — a parabola.

Key Properties of the Parabola
  • Graph of \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) is always parabolic.
  • Axis of the parabola: \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\) (vertical line, parallel to Y-axis).
  • Vertex: \(\left(-\dfrac{b}{2a},\; -\dfrac{D}{4a}\right)\)
  • If \(a > 0\): parabola opens upward \(\cup\)
  • If \(a < 0\): parabola opens downward \(\cap\)
Position of Parabola w.r.t. X-axis (Discriminant \(D = b^2 - 4ac\))
ConditionPositionX-intercepts
\(D > 0\) Cuts X-axis at two distinct points \(x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}\)
\(D = 0\) Touches X-axis at one point (vertex on X-axis) \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\)
\(D < 0\) Does not cut X-axis (entirely above or below) No real x-intercepts

Maximum & Minimum Values

\(a > 0\): Minimum Value

The quadratic expression has a minimum (least value) at \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\):

\[\text{Minimum value} = -\frac{D}{4a} = \frac{4ac - b^2}{4a}\]

There is no greatest value (expression → ∞ as \(x \to \pm\infty\)).

\(a < 0\): Maximum Value

The quadratic expression has a maximum (greatest value) at \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\):

\[\text{Maximum value} = -\frac{D}{4a} = \frac{4ac - b^2}{4a}\]

There is no least value (expression → −∞ as \(x \to \pm\infty\)).

Condition for \(f(x)\) to be Positive Definite / Negative Definite
  • Positive definite (\(f(x) > 0\) for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\)): \(a > 0\) and \(D < 0\)
  • Non-negative definite (\(f(x) \ge 0\) for all \(x\)): \(a > 0\) and \(D \le 0\)
  • Negative definite (\(f(x) < 0\) for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\)): \(a < 0\) and \(D < 0\)
  • Non-positive definite (\(f(x) \le 0\) for all \(x\)): \(a < 0\) and \(D \le 0\)

Position of Roots in an Interval

For \(f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c\), let \(D = b^2 - 4ac\) and vertex \(x_v = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\). The conditions for the roots \(\alpha, \beta\) to satisfy various location criteria:

Root Location Conditions (with \(a > 0\) shown)
#RequirementConditions
(i) Both roots positive (in \((0,\infty)\)) \(D \ge 0\),   \(-\dfrac{b}{a} > 0\) (sum +ve),   \(\dfrac{c}{a} > 0\) (product +ve)
(ii) Both roots negative (in \((-\infty, 0)\)) \(D \ge 0\),   \(-\dfrac{b}{a} < 0\) (sum −ve),   \(\dfrac{c}{a} > 0\) (product +ve)
(iii) Both roots greater than \(k\) \(D \ge 0\),   \(x_v > k\),   \(a\,f(k) > 0\)
(iv) Both roots less than \(k\) \(D \ge 0\),   \(x_v < k\),   \(a\,f(k) > 0\)
(v) Both roots lie in \((k_1, k_2)\) \(D \ge 0\),   \(k_1 < x_v < k_2\),   \(a\,f(k_1) > 0\),   \(a\,f(k_2) > 0\)
(vi) Exactly one root in \((k_1, k_2)\) \(D > 0\) and \(f(k_1) \cdot f(k_2) < 0\)
(vii) Given \(k\) lies between the roots \(D > 0\) and \(a\,f(k) < 0\)
(viii) Roots of opposite sign (0 between roots) \(D > 0\) and \(a\,f(0) < 0\), i.e. \(\dfrac{c}{a} < 0\)

Quadratic Equation — Roots & Discriminant

For the equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) (\(a \ne 0\)), the roots are:

\[\alpha,\,\beta = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}, \quad D = b^2 - 4ac\]
Vieta's Formulae & Symmetric Expressions
\[\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}, \qquad |\alpha - \beta| = \frac{\sqrt{D}}{|a|}\]

Equation from roots: \(x^2 - (\alpha+\beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0\)

ExpressionValue in terms of \(a, b, c\)
\(\alpha^2 + \beta^2\)\(\dfrac{b^2 - 2ac}{a^2}\)
\((\alpha - \beta)^2\)\(\dfrac{D}{a^2}\)
\(\alpha^3 + \beta^3\)\(\dfrac{-b(b^2 - 3ac)}{a^3}\)
\(\alpha^2 + \alpha\beta + \beta^2\)\(\dfrac{b^2 - ac}{a^2}\)
\(\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta} + \dfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\)\(\dfrac{b^2 - 2ac}{ac}\)
\(\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2\)\(-\dfrac{bc}{a^2}\)

Special Conditions on Roots

Common Root Conditions

For equations \(a_1x^2+b_1x+c_1=0\) and \(a_2x^2+b_2x+c_2=0\):

  • Exactly one common root: \[(c_1a_2 - c_2a_1)^2 = (b_1c_2 - b_2c_1)(a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)\]
  • Both roots common: \[\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}\]
Roots in the Same Ratio & One Root \(k\) Times Another
  • If roots of \(a_1x^2+b_1x+c_1=0\) and \(a_2x^2+b_2x+c_2=0\) are in the same ratio: \[\frac{b_1^2}{a_1c_1} = \frac{b_2^2}{a_2c_2}\]
  • If one root of \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) is \(k\) times the other: \[\frac{(k+1)^2}{k} = \frac{b^2}{ac}\]
Condition for Quadratic Function to be Always Resolvable into Linear Factors

The function \(f(x,y) = ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c\) is always resolvable into linear factors iff:

\[\Delta = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix} = abc + 2fgh - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 = 0\]
