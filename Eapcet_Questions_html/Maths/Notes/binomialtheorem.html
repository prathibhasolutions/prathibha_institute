Binomial Theorem & Principle of Mathematical Induction

Binomial Expression

An algebraic expression consisting of two terms with a positive or negative sign between them is called a binomial expression.

Examples: \( (x + a) \), \( (2x - 3y) \), \( \left(x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right) \)

Binomial Theorem for Positive Integer

If \( n \) is any positive integer, then:

\[ (x + a)^n = \binom{n}{0}x^n + \binom{n}{1}x^{n-1}a + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}a^2 + \cdots + \binom{n}{n}a^n \]

i.e., \(\displaystyle (x+a)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} x^{n-r} a^r \)

where \( x \) and \( a \) are real numbers, and \( \binom{n}{0}, \binom{n}{1}, \binom{n}{2}, \ldots, \binom{n}{n} \) are called binomial coefficients.

Also, \(\displaystyle \binom{n}{r} = {}^nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \) for \( 0 \le r \le n \).

Properties of Binomial Theorem for Positive Integer
  • Total number of terms in the expansion of \( (x+a)^n \) is \( (n+1) \), i.e., a finite number of terms.
  • The sum of the indices of \( x \) and \( a \) in each term is \( n \).
  • The expansion is also true when \( x \) and \( a \) are complex numbers.
  • The coefficients of terms equidistant from the beginning and end are equal: \( {}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n-r} \).
  • The values of the binomial coefficients steadily increase to a maximum and then steadily decrease.
  • In the expansion of \( (x+a)^n \), the \( r \)-th term from the end is \( (n-r+2) \)-th term from the beginning.
  • If \( n \) is a positive integer, then the number of terms in \( (x+y+z)^n \) is \( \dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2} \).
Special Cases

(i) \(\displaystyle (x-a)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \cdot x^{n-r} \cdot a^r \)

(ii) \(\displaystyle (1+x)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} {}^nC_r \cdot x^r = {}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 x + {}^nC_2 x^2 + \cdots + {}^nC_n x^n \)

(iii) \(\displaystyle (1-x)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \cdot x^r \)

(iv) The coefficient of \( x^r \) in \( (1+x)^n \) is \( {}^nC_r \) and in \( (1-x)^n \) is \( (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \).

(v) \[ (x+a)^n + (x-a)^n = 2\!\left[{}^nC_0 x^n a^0 + {}^nC_2 x^{n-2}a^2 + {}^nC_4 x^{n-4}a^4 + \cdots \right] \] \[ (x+a)^n - (x-a)^n = 2\!\left[{}^nC_1 x^{n-1}a + {}^nC_3 x^{n-3}a^3 + \cdots \right] \]

(vi)

  • If \( n \) is odd, then \( (x+a)^n \) and \( (x-a)^n \) both have the same number of terms equal to \( \dfrac{n+1}{2} \).
  • If \( n \) is even, then \( (x+a)^n + (x-a)^n \) has \( \dfrac{n}{2}+1 \) terms and \( (x+a)^n - (x-a)^n \) has \( \dfrac{n}{2} \) terms.

General Term in a Binomial Expansion

(i) General term in the expansion of \( (x+a)^n \):

\[ T_{r+1} = {}^nC_r \cdot x^{n-r} \cdot a^r \]

(ii) General term in the expansion of \( (x-a)^n \):

\[ T_{r+1} = (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \cdot x^{n-r} \cdot a^r \]

(iii) General term in the expansion of \( (1+x)^n \):

\[ T_{r+1} = {}^nC_r \cdot x^r \]

(iv) General term in the expansion of \( (1-x)^n \):

\[ T_{r+1} = (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \cdot x^r \]

Some Important Results
  • Coefficient of \( x^m \) in the expansion of \( \left(ax^p + \dfrac{b}{x^q}\right)^n \) is the coefficient of \( T_{r+1} \), where \( r = \dfrac{np - m}{p+q} \).
  • The term independent of \( x \) in the expansion of \( \left(ax^p + \dfrac{b}{x^q}\right)^n \) is \( T_{r+1} \), where \( r = \dfrac{np}{p+q} \).
  • If the coefficients of \( r \)-th, \( (r+1) \)-th and \( (r+2) \)-th terms of \( (1+x)^n \) are in AP, then \( n^2 - 4r + 4n - 2 = 0 \) or \( n^2 - (4r-1)n + 4r^2 - 2 = 0 \).
  • In the expansion of \( (1+x)^n \): \( \dfrac{T_{r+1}}{T_r} = \dfrac{n-r+1}{r} \).
  • Coefficient of \( x^{n-1} \) in the expansion of \( (x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n) = -\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} \).
  • Coefficient of \( x^{n-1} \) in the expansion of \( (x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+n) = \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} \).
  • If the coefficients of \( p \)-th and \( q \)-th terms in the expansion of \( (1+x)^n \) are equal, then \( p + q = n + 2 \).
  • If the coefficients of \( x^r \) and \( x^{r+1} \) in \( \left(a + \dfrac{x}{b}\right)^n \) are equal, then \( n = (r+1)(ab+1) - 1 \).
  • Number of terms in the expansion of \( (x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_r)^n \) is \( {}^{n+r-1}C_{r-1} \).

Middle Term in a Binomial Expansion

  • If \( n \) is even in \( (x+a)^n \) or \( (x-a)^n \), then the middle term is the \( \left(\dfrac{n}{2}+1\right) \)-th term.
  • If \( n \) is odd in \( (x+a)^n \) or \( (x-a)^n \), then there are two middle terms: the \( \left(\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right) \)-th term and the \( \left(\dfrac{n+3}{2}\right) \)-th term.
Note: When there are two middle terms in the expansion, their binomial coefficients are equal.

Greatest Coefficient & Greatest Term

Greatest Coefficient

The binomial coefficient of the middle term is the greatest binomial coefficient.

  • If \( n \) is even, the greatest coefficient in \( (x+a)^n \) is \( {}^nC_{n/2} \).
  • If \( n \) is odd, the greatest coefficient in \( (x+a)^n \) is \( {}^nC_{(n-1)/2} \) or \( {}^nC_{(n+1)/2} \).
Greatest Term

In the expansion of \( (x+a)^n \):

  • If \( \dfrac{(n+1)\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|}{1+\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|} \) is an integer \( = p \) (say), then the greatest terms are \( T_p \) and \( T_{p+1} \).
  • If \( \dfrac{(n+1)\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|}{1+\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|} \) is not an integer with \( m \) as its integral part, then \( T_{m+1} \) is the greatest term.

Divisibility Problems

From the expansion \( (1+x)^n = 1 + {}^nC_1 x + {}^nC_2 x^2 + \cdots + {}^nC_n x^n \), we conclude:

  • \( (1+x)^n - 1 = {}^nC_1 x + {}^nC_2 x^2 + \cdots + {}^nC_n x^n \) is divisible by \( x \), i.e., it is a multiple of \( x \). This can be written as \( M(x) \).
  • \( (1+x)^n - 1 - nx = {}^nC_2 x^2 + {}^nC_3 x^3 + \cdots = M(x^2) \) is divisible by \( x^2 \).
  • \( (1+x)^n - 1 - nx - \binom{n}{2}x^2 = {}^nC_3 x^3 + {}^nC_4 x^4 + \cdots = M(x^3) \) is divisible by \( x^3 \).

Important Results on Binomial Coefficients

If \( C_0, C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_n \) are the coefficients of \( (1+x)^n \), then:

Basic Relations
  • \( {}^nC_r + {}^nC_{r-1} = {}^{n+1}C_r \)
  • \( \dfrac{{}^nC_r}{{}^nC_{r-1}} = \dfrac{n-r+1}{r} \)
  • \( \dfrac{{}^nC_r}{{}^{n-1}C_r} = \dfrac{n}{n-r} \)
  • \( C_0 + C_1 + C_2 + \cdots + C_n = 2^n \)
  • \( C_0 + C_2 + C_4 + \cdots = C_1 + C_3 + C_5 + \cdots = 2^{n-1} \)
Trigonometric & Algebraic Results
  • \( C_0 - C_2 + C_4 - C_6 + \cdots = \left(\sqrt{2}\right)^n \cos\dfrac{n\pi}{4} \)
  • \( C_1 - C_3 + C_5 - C_7 + \cdots = \left(\sqrt{2}\right)^n \sin\dfrac{n\pi}{4} \)
  • \( C_0 - C_1 + C_2 - C_3 + \cdots + (-1)^n C_n = 0 \)
  • \( C_1 + 2C_2 + 3C_3 + \cdots + nC_n = n \cdot 2^{n-1} \)
  • \( C_0 \cdot 3 + C_1 \cdot 2 + C_2 \cdot 1 + \cdots + (-1)^n(n+1)C_n = 0 \) (when \( n \geq 2 \))
  • \( C_0 C_r + C_1 C_{r+1} + \cdots + C_{n-r}C_n = {}^{2n}C_{n-r} = \dfrac{(2n)!}{(n-r)!(n+r)!} \)
Sum of Squares and Ratios

(xii) \(\displaystyle C_0^2 + C_1^2 + C_2^2 + \cdots + C_n^2 = {}^{2n}C_n = \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2} \)

(xiii) \(\displaystyle C_0^2 - C_1^2 + C_2^2 - C_3^2 + \cdots + (-1)^n C_n^2\)

ConditionValue
\( n \) is odd \( = \ 0 \)
\( n \) is even \(\displaystyle = \ (-1)^{n/2} \cdot {}^{n}C_{n/2} \)

(xiv) \(\displaystyle \frac{C_1}{2} - \frac{C_2}{2^2} + \frac{C_3}{3} - \cdots + (-1)^{n-1} \cdot \frac{n \cdot C_n}{} \)

ConditionValue
\( n \) is odd \( = \ 0 \)
\( n \) is even \(\displaystyle = \ (-1)^{n/2 - 1} \cdot \frac{n}{2} \cdot {}^{n}C_{n/2} \)

(xv) \(\displaystyle C_0 + \frac{C_1}{2} + \frac{C_2}{3} + \cdots + \frac{C_n}{n+1} = \frac{2^{n+1}-1}{n+1} \)

(xvi) \(\displaystyle C_0 - \frac{C_1}{2} + \frac{C_2}{3} - \frac{C_3}{4} + \cdots + (-1)^n \frac{C_n}{n+1} = \frac{1}{n+1} \)

(xvii) \(\displaystyle C_0 + \frac{C_1}{2^2} + \frac{C_2}{3 \cdot 2^2} + \frac{C_3}{4 \cdot 2^3} + \cdots + \frac{C_n}{(n+1) \cdot 2^n} = \frac{3^{n+1}-1}{(n+1)\cdot 2^n} \)

(xviii) \[\displaystyle \sum_{r=0}^{n} (-1)^r \cdot {}^nC_r \cdot \left(\frac{1}{r+2}\right) = \frac{1}{n+2} \cdot \left(\frac{2^{mn} - (2^m - 1)^n}{1}\right)\]

Multinomial Theorem

For any \( n \in \mathbb{N} \):

(i) \(\displaystyle (x_1 + x_2)^n = \sum_{r_1+r_2=n} \frac{n!}{r_1!\, r_2!}\, x_1^{r_1} x_2^{r_2} \)

(ii) \(\displaystyle (x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_k)^n = \sum_{r_1+r_2+\cdots+r_k=n} \frac{n!}{r_1!\, r_2!\, \cdots\, r_k!}\, x_1^{r_1} x_2^{r_2} \cdots x_k^{r_k} \)

(iii) The general term in the above expansion is: \[ \frac{n!}{r_1!\, r_2!\, \cdots\, r_k!}\; x_1^{r_1}\, x_2^{r_2}\, \cdots\, x_k^{r_k} \]

(iv) The greatest coefficient in the expansion of \( (x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_m)^n \) is: \[ \frac{n!}{(q!)^{m-r}\, [(q+1)!]^r} \] where \( q \) and \( r \) are the quotient and remainder respectively when \( n \) is divided by \( m \).

Important Results
  • If \( n \) is a positive integer and \( a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m \in \mathbb{R} \), then the coefficient of \( x^r \) in the expansion of \( (a_1 + a_2 x + a_3 x^2 + \cdots + a_m x^{m-1})^n \) is \( \displaystyle \sum \frac{n!}{n_1!\, n_2!\, \cdots\, n_m!}\, a_1^{n_1} a_2^{n_2} \cdots a_m^{n_m} \).
  • Total number of terms in the expansion of \( (a + b + c + d)^n \) is \( \dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{6} \).

R-f Factor Relations

If \( (\sqrt{A} + \sqrt{B})^n = I + f \), where \( I \) is a positive integer and \( 0 \le f < 1 \), and let \( f' = (\sqrt{A} - \sqrt{B})^n \) where \( A - B = k > 0 \) and \( 0 < f' < 1 \), then:

  • When \( n \) is odd: \( (I + f) + f' = I + f + f' \) and \( f \cdot f' = k^n \).
  • When \( n \) is even: \( f = f' \) and \( (I + f)(f') = k^n \), i.e., \( (I + f)(1 - f) = k^n \).

Binomial Theorem for Any Index (Rational / Negative)

If \( n \) is any rational number and \( |x| < 1 \), then:

\[ (1+x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{1 \cdot 2}x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}x^3 + \cdots \]

Important Notes
  • If \( n \) is a positive integer, the series on the RHS is finite. If \( n \) is a negative or rational number, there are infinitely many terms.
  • General term in the expansion of \( (1+x)^n \): \[ T_{r+1} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!}\, x^r \]
Expansion of \( (x+a)^n \) for Rational Index

Case I — When \( x > a \) (i.e., \( \left|\frac{a}{x}\right| < 1 \)):

\[ (x+a)^n = x^n\!\left(1+\frac{a}{x}\right)^n = x^n\!\left[1 + n\cdot\frac{a}{x} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}\left(\frac{a}{x}\right)^{\!2} + \cdots \right] \]

Case II — When \( x < a \) (i.e., \( \left|\frac{x}{a}\right| < 1 \)):

\[ (x+a)^n = a^n\!\left(1+\frac{x}{a}\right)^n = a^n\!\left[1 + n\cdot\frac{x}{a} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^{\!2} + \cdots \right] \]

Standard Expansions \( (|x| < 1) \)
  • \( (1-x)^{-1} = 1 + x + x^2 + x^3 + \cdots + x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1+x)^{-1} = 1 - x + x^2 - x^3 + \cdots + (-1)^r x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1-x)^{-2} = 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \cdots + (r+1)x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1+x)^{-2} = 1 - 2x + 3x^2 - 4x^3 + \cdots + (-1)^r(r+1)x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1-x)^{-3} = 1 + 3x + 6x^2 + 10x^3 + \cdots + \dfrac{(r+1)(r+2)}{2}x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1+x)^{-3} = 1 - 3x + 6x^2 - 10x^3 + \cdots + (-1)^r \dfrac{(r+1)(r+2)}{2}x^r + \cdots \)
  • \( (1+x)^n \approx 1 + nx \) if \( x, x^2, x^3, \ldots \) are all very small compared to 1.

Principle of Mathematical Induction

In algebra, there are certain results formulated in terms of \( n \), where \( n \) is a positive integer. Such results can be proved by a specific technique known as the Principle of Mathematical Induction.

Statement

A sentence or description which can be judged as either true or false is called a statement.

Examples:

  • 3 divides 9. True
  • Lucknow is the capital of Uttar Pradesh. True
First Principle of Mathematical Induction

Let \( P(n) \) be a statement involving natural number \( n \). To prove \( P(n) \) is true for all natural numbers:

  1. Prove that \( P(1) \) is true. (Base case)
  2. Assume \( P(k) \) is true for some arbitrary positive integer \( k \). (Inductive hypothesis)
  3. Using steps (i) and (ii), prove that \( P(k+1) \) is true. (Inductive step)

By the First Principle of Mathematical Induction, \( P(n) \) is true for all \( n \in \mathbb{N} \).

Second Principle of Mathematical Induction

In the second principle, the following steps are used:

  1. Prove that \( P(1) \) is true.
  2. Assume \( P(n) \) is true for all natural numbers \( n \) such that \( 2 \le n < k \).
  3. Using (i) and (ii), prove that \( P(k+1) \) is true.

This is sometimes called the Strong Induction or Complete Induction principle.

Common Results Proved by Mathematical Induction
  • \( 1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + n = \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} \)
  • \( 1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + \cdots + n^2 = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \)
  • \( 1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + \cdots + n^3 = \left[\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\right]^2 \)
  • \( 2^n > n \) for all \( n \geq 1 \)
  • \( n! > 2^n \) for all \( n \geq 4 \)
  • \( (1+x)^n \geq 1 + nx \) for \( x > -1 \) (Bernoulli's Inequality)
