Statistics — Measures of Dispersion

Basic Terms & Frequency Distributions

Statistics is the science of collection, organisation, presentation, analysis, and interpretation of numerical data.

Key Definitions
  • Primary Data: Collected by the investigator directly.
  • Secondary Data: Obtained from some existing source.
  • Variable/Variate: A characteristic that varies in magnitude (e.g. weight, height).
  • Class Interval: Groups used to condense data.
  • Class Limits: Lower and upper values of each class.
  • Class Mark: \(\dfrac{\text{Lower limit} + \text{Upper limit}}{2}\)
  • Class Width: Upper boundary \(-\) Lower boundary.
  • Frequency: Number of times an observation occurs.
  • Cumulative Frequency: Running total of frequencies (less than / more than type).
Graphical Representations
  • Bar Diagram: Heights of bars proportional to frequencies.
  • Histogram: Rectangles on class intervals; area proportional to frequency (height \(\propto\) frequency/class width for unequal widths).
  • Pie Diagram: Central angle \(= \dfrac{\text{Frequency}}{\text{Total frequency}} \times 360°\)
  • Frequency Polygon: Points (class mark, frequency) joined by straight lines.
  • Ogive (Cumulative Frequency Curve): 'Less than' ogive or 'More than' ogive. Their intersection gives the median.

Measures of Central Tendency

1. Arithmetic Mean (AM)
  • Raw data: \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum x_i}{n}\)
  • Discrete frequency distribution: \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\)
  • Grouped data (direct): \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\) (\(x_i\) = class mark)
  • Shortcut method: \(\bar{x} = A_1 + \dfrac{\sum f_i d_i}{\sum f_i}\), where \(d_i = x_i - A_1\)
  • Step deviation method: \(\bar{x} = A_1 + \dfrac{\sum f_i u_i}{\sum f_i} \times h\), where \(u_i = \dfrac{x_i - A_1}{h}\)
  • Combined mean: \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{n_1A_1 + n_2A_2 + \cdots + n_rA_r}{n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_r}\)
  • Weighted AM: \(A_w = \dfrac{\sum w_i x_i}{\sum w_i}\)

Properties: Algebraic sum of deviations from mean \(= 0\). Sum of squares of deviations is minimum when taken about the mean.

2. Geometric Mean (GM)
  • Raw data: \(G = (x_1 \cdot x_2 \cdots x_n)^{1/n}\), or \(G = \text{antilog}\!\left(\dfrac{\sum\log x_i}{n}\right)\)
  • Frequency distribution: \(G = \text{antilog}\!\left(\dfrac{\sum f_i \log x_i}{N}\right)\) where \(N = \sum f_i\)
3. Harmonic Mean (HM)
  • Raw data: \(\text{HM} = \dfrac{n}{\sum\frac{1}{x_i}}\)
  • Frequency distribution: \(\text{HM} = \dfrac{\sum f_i}{\sum\frac{f_i}{x_i}}\)
4. Median
  • Raw data (sorted): If \(n\) is odd → \(\left(\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right)\)th term. If \(n\) is even → mean of \(\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)\)th and \(\left(\dfrac{n}{2}+1\right)\)th terms.
  • Grouped data: \[M_d = l + \frac{\dfrac{N}{2} - C}{f} \times h\] where \(l\) = lower limit of median class, \(f\) = its frequency, \(C\) = cumulative frequency before median class, \(h\) = class width, \(N = \sum f_i\).
  • Quartiles (grouped): \[Q_1 = l + \frac{\dfrac{N}{4}-C}{f}\times h, \qquad Q_3 = l + \frac{\dfrac{3N}{4}-C}{f}\times h\]
  • Inter-quartile range \(= Q_3 - Q_1\)
  • Quartile deviation \(= \dfrac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}\)
  • Coefficient of quartile deviation \(= \dfrac{Q_3-Q_1}{Q_3+Q_1}\)
5. Mode
  • Raw data: Value that appears most frequently.
  • Discrete frequency: Value with maximum frequency.
  • Grouped data: \[M_O = l + \frac{f_0 - f_1}{2f_0 - f_1 - f_2} \times h\] where \(l\) = lower limit of modal class, \(f_0\) = frequency of modal class, \(f_1\) = pre-modal class frequency, \(f_2\) = post-modal class frequency, \(h\) = class width.

Relation between Mean, Median, Mode (empirical formula):

\[\text{Mode} = 3\,\text{Median} - 2\,\text{Mean}\]

In a symmetric distribution: Mean \(=\) Median \(=\) Mode.

Measures of Dispersion

The degree to which data tends to spread about an average value is called dispersion.

1. Range
\[\text{Range} = X_{\max} - X_{\min}\] \[\text{Coefficient of Range} = \frac{X_{\max} - X_{\min}}{X_{\max} + X_{\min}}\]
2. Mean Deviation (MD)

Arithmetic mean of absolute deviations from a central value (\(\bar{x}\), \(M_d\), or \(M_O\)):

  • Raw data: \(\delta = \dfrac{\sum|x_i - \bar{x}|}{n}\)
  • Discrete frequency: \(\delta = \dfrac{\sum f_i|x_i - \bar{x}|}{\sum f_i}\)
  • Grouped data: \(\delta = \dfrac{\sum f_i|x_i - \bar{x}|}{\sum f_i}\) (\(x_i\) = class mark)

Coefficient of MD: \(\dfrac{\delta}{\bar{x}}\) or \(\dfrac{\delta}{M_d}\) or \(\dfrac{\delta}{M_O}\)

Note: MD is least when measured from the median.

3. Standard Deviation (SD) & Variance

Variance \(\sigma^2\) and SD \(\sigma\):

  • Raw data: \[\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x_i^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2}\]
  • Discrete frequency: \[\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{N}} = \frac{1}{N}\sqrt{N\sum f_ix_i^2 - \left(\sum f_ix_i\right)^2}\] Shortcut: \(\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{\sum f_i d_i^2}{N} - \left(\dfrac{\sum f_i d_i}{N}\right)^2}\), where \(d_i = x_i - A\)
  • Grouped data (step deviation): \[\sigma = h\sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N}\right)^2}, \quad u_i = \frac{x_i-A}{h}\]
  • Combined SD: \(\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{n_1(\sigma_1^2+d_1^2)+n_2(\sigma_2^2+d_2^2)}{n_1+n_2}}\), where \(d_1 = X_1 - \bar{X}\), \(d_2 = X_2 - \bar{X}\), \(\bar{X} = \dfrac{n_1X_1+n_2X_2}{n_1+n_2}\)
4. Root Mean Square (RMS) Deviation

Square root of AM of squared deviations from assumed mean \(A'\):

  • Raw data: \(S = \sqrt{\dfrac{\sum(x_i-A')^2}{n}}\)
  • Frequency distribution: \(S = \sqrt{\dfrac{\sum f_i(x_i-A')^2}{\sum f_i}}\)

Note: If \(A' = \bar{x}\), then \(S = \sigma\). RMS deviation is least when measured from AM.

Relation: \(\sigma^2 = S^2 - (A' - \bar{x})^2\), i.e. \(\sigma^2 + \bar{x}^2 = \dfrac{\sum f_ix_i^2}{\sum f_i}\)

  • Coefficient of variation (CV): \(\dfrac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100\). More consistent distribution has smaller CV.
  • SD of first \(n\) natural numbers: \(\sqrt{\dfrac{n^2-1}{12}}\)
  • SD is independent of change of origin, but depends on change of scale.
  • Quartile deviation \(= \dfrac{2}{3}\,\sigma\) and Mean deviation \(= \dfrac{4}{5}\,\sigma\) (approximately, for normal distribution).
  • MD about mean \(\le \sigma\).

Correlation

Correlation measures the degree of simultaneous variation between two variables. Types: Perfect, Positive (direct), Negative (indirect).

Covariance
\[\text{cov}(x,y) = \frac{1}{n}\sum(x_i-\bar{x})(y_i-\bar{y}) = \frac{\sum x_iy_i}{n} - \bar{x}\,\bar{y}\]
Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Correlation
\[r(x,y) = \frac{\text{cov}(x,y)}{\sigma_x\,\sigma_y} = \frac{\sum(x_i-\bar{x})(y_i-\bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\bar{x})^2\cdot\sum(y_i-\bar{y})^2}}\] \[= \frac{n\sum x_iy_i - \sum x_i\sum y_i}{\sqrt{n\sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}\cdot\sqrt{n\sum y_i^2-(\sum y_i)^2}}\]

Properties:

  • \(-1 \le r \le 1\)
  • \(r = 1\): perfect positive; \(r = -1\): perfect negative; \(r = 0\): uncorrelated.
  • Independent of change of origin and scale.
  • No units (pure number).
  • If \(x\) and \(y\) are independent, \(r = 0\) (converse need not hold).
  • For constants \(a, c \ne 0\): \(r(ax+b, cy+d) = \dfrac{|ac|}{ac}\cdot r(x,y)\)
  • Probable error \(= 0.6745\dfrac{1-r^2}{\sqrt{n}}\)
Spearman's Rank Correlation

Let \(d_i\) = difference of paired ranks, \(n\) = number of items.

  • Ranks not repeated: \[r = 1 - \frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}\]
  • Ranks repeated (with correction factor for each repeated rank of multiplicity \(m\)): \[r = 1 - \frac{6\!\left[\sum d_i^2 + \frac{1}{12}\sum(m_i^3-m_i)\right]}{n(n^2-1)}\]

Rank correlation coefficient lies between \(-1\) and \(1\).

Regression

Regression estimates the unknown value of one variable from known values of another related variable. A line of regression gives the best fit in the least-squares sense.

Regression Equations & Coefficients
  • Line of regression of \(y\) on \(x\): \[y - \bar{y} = b_{yx}(x - \bar{x}), \qquad b_{yx} = r\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = \frac{\text{cov}(x,y)}{\sigma_x^2}\]
  • Line of regression of \(x\) on \(y\): \[x - \bar{x} = b_{xy}(y - \bar{y}), \qquad b_{xy} = r\frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = \frac{\text{cov}(x,y)}{\sigma_y^2}\]
  • Angle between two regression lines: \[\tan\theta = \frac{1-r^2}{r}\cdot\frac{\sigma_x\sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2}\]
    • If \(r = 0\): lines are perpendicular (\(\theta = \pi/2\)).
    • If \(|r| = 1\): lines coincide (\(\theta = 0\)).
Properties of Regression Coefficients
  • Both \(b_{xy}\), \(b_{yx}\), and \(r\) have the same sign.
  • Correlation coefficient is the geometric mean of regression coefficients: \[r = \sqrt{b_{xy} \cdot b_{yx}}\]
  • \(0 \le |b_{xy}| \cdot |b_{yx}| \le 1\). If \(|b_{xy}| > 1\), then \(|b_{yx}| < 1\).
  • Regression coefficients are independent of change of origin but not of scale.
  • If the two regression coefficients have different signs, then \(r = 0\).
  • AM of regression coefficients \(\ge r\): \(\dfrac{b_{yx}+b_{xy}}{2} \ge r\)
