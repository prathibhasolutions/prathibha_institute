Differential Equations

Introduction — Order, Degree & Types

A differential equation involves an independent variable, a dependent variable, and differential coefficients of the dependent variable w.r.t. the independent variable.

  • Order: The order of the highest derivative in the equation (always a positive integer).
  • Degree: The exponent of the highest-order derivative, when the equation is a polynomial in derivatives. (Not defined if the equation is not polynomial in derivatives — e.g. \(\cos\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0\).)

Example: \(\left(\dfrac{d^3y}{dx^3}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^3 = 0\) has order 3, degree 2.

Linear vs Non-Linear

A DE is linear if the dependent variable and all its derivatives appear in the first power with no products between them. General linear DE of order \(n\):

\[P_0\frac{d^ny}{dx^n} + P_1\frac{d^{n-1}y}{dx^{n-1}} + \cdots + P_{n-1}\frac{dy}{dx} + P_ny = Q\]

where \(P_0, P_1, \ldots, P_n\) and \(Q\) are functions of \(x\) only (or constants).

A linear DE is always of the first degree, but a first-degree DE need not be linear.

General & Particular Solution
  • General Solution: Contains as many arbitrary constants as the order of the DE.
  • Particular Solution: Obtained by assigning specific values to the arbitrary constants in the general solution.

Formation: To form a DE from \(f(x, y, c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_n) = 0\), differentiate \(n\) times and eliminate the \(n\) constants.

Methods of Solution — First Order & First Degree

1. Exact Differential Form

If the DE can be written as \(f_1(x,y)\,d[f_1(x,y)] + f_2(x,y)\,d[f_2(x,y)] + \cdots = 0\), integrate each term separately. Key exact differentials:

ExpressionExact Differential
\(x\,dy + y\,dx\)\(d(xy)\)
\(x\,dy - y\,dx\)\(d\!\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) \cdot x^2\) or \(d\!\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right) \cdot (-y^2)\)
\(dx + dy\)\(d(x+y)\)
\(\dfrac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2+y^2}\)\(d\!\left(\tan^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}\right)\)
\(\dfrac{y\,dx - x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\)\(d\!\left(\tan^{-1}\dfrac{x}{y}\right)\)
\(\dfrac{x\,dx + y\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\)\(d\!\left(\dfrac{\log(x^2+y^2)}{2}\right)\)
\(\dfrac{x\,dy + y\,dx}{xy}\)\(d(\log xy)\)
\(\dfrac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{xy}\)\(d\!\left(\log\dfrac{y}{x}\right)\)
\(\dfrac{-y\,dx + x\,dy}{x^2-y^2}\)\(d\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\log\dfrac{x+y}{x-y}\right)\)
\(\dfrac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2-y^2}\)\(d\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left|\dfrac{x-y}{x+y}\right|\right)\)
\(x\,dx + y\,dy\)\(d\!\left(\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{2}\right)\)
\(x^{m-1}y^{n-1}(my\,dx + nx\,dy)\)\(d(x^m y^n)\)
\(ye^x\,dx + e^x\,dy\)\(d(ye^x)\)
\(xe^y\,dy + e^y\,dx\)\(d(xe^y)\)

Also: \(\dfrac{f'(x,y)}{[f(x,y)]^n}\,d[f(x,y)] = d\!\left[\dfrac{[f(x,y)]^{1-n}}{1-n}\right]\) and \(\dfrac{dx}{x^2} - \dfrac{dy}{y^2} = d\!\left(\dfrac{1}{y} - \dfrac{1}{x}\right)\)

2. Variables Separable

If the DE reduces to \(f(x)\,dx = g(y)\,dy\), integrate both sides:

\[\int f(x)\,dx = \int g(y)\,dy + C\]
3. Reducible to Variables Separable — \(dy/dx = f(ax+by+c)\)

Substitute \(z = ax + by + c \Rightarrow \dfrac{dz}{dx} = a + b\dfrac{dy}{dx}\). The equation becomes:

\[\frac{dz}{dx} = a + b\,f(z) \implies \frac{dz}{bf(z)+a} = dx\]

which is now separable.

4. Homogeneous Differential Equation

DE of the form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{g(x,y)}\) where \(f\) and \(g\) are homogeneous of the same degree, i.e. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = F\!\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)\).

Solution: Put \(y = vx \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\). Equation becomes:

\[v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = F(v) \implies \frac{dv}{F(v)-v} = \frac{dx}{x}\]

which is separable. Similarly for \(\dfrac{dx}{dy} = G\!\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)\), put \(x = vy\).

Note: \(f(x,y)\) is homogeneous of degree \(n\) if \(f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \lambda^n f(x,y)\).

5. Reducible to Homogeneous Form

For \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{a_1x+b_1y+c_1}{a_2x+b_2y+c_2}\) with \(\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \ne \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\):

Put \(x = X+h\), \(y = Y+k\) and choose \(h, k\) to make constant terms vanish:

\[h = \frac{b_1c_2 - b_2c_1}{a_1b_2 - a_2b_1}, \qquad k = \frac{c_1a_2 - c_2a_1}{a_1b_2 - a_2b_1}\]

Equation becomes homogeneous in \(X, Y\).

6. Linear Differential Equation

Form (i): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q\), where \(P, Q\) are functions of \(x\).

\[\text{Integrating Factor (IF)} = e^{\int P\,dx}\] \[\text{Solution: } y\cdot\text{IF} = \int Q\cdot\text{IF}\,dx + C\]

Form (ii): \(\dfrac{dx}{dy} + Rx = S\), where \(R, S\) are functions of \(y\).

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int R\,dy}, \qquad x\cdot\text{IF} = \int S\cdot\text{IF}\,dy + C\]
7. Reducible to Linear Form

Equation of the form \(f'(y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} + f(y)P = Q\) (where \(P, Q\) are functions of \(x\)):

Substitute \(u = f(y) \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = f'(y)\dfrac{dy}{dx}\). Becomes \(\dfrac{du}{dx} + Pu = Q\) — linear in \(u\).

8. Bernoulli's Equation

Form: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Qy^n\) \((n \ne 0, 1)\), where \(P, Q\) are functions of \(x\).

Divide by \(y^n\), then put \(z = y^{1-n} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dz}{dx} = (1-n)y^{-n}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\). Equation becomes:

\[\frac{dz}{dx} + (1-n)Pz = (1-n)Q\] which is linear in \(z\).

Orthogonal Trajectory

A curve that cuts every member of a given family of curves at right angles is called an orthogonal trajectory of that family.

Procedure:

  1. Let \(f(x, y, c) = 0\) be the given family. Differentiate w.r.t. \(x\) and eliminate \(c\) to get the DE of the family.
  2. Replace \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) by \(-\dfrac{dx}{dy}\) in the DE.
  3. Solve the resulting DE to get the orthogonal trajectories.
