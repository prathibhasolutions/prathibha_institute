Probability

Experiments & Basic Definitions

An experiment is an operation which produces well-defined results or outcomes.

Types of Experiments
  • Deterministic Experiment: When repeated under identical conditions, always produces the same result.
  • Probabilistic / Random Experiment: When repeated under identical conditions, may produce different outcomes — but every outcome is one of several possible outcomes.
Key Definitions
  • Trial: Performing an experiment once.
  • Sample Space (\(S\)): The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.
  • Sample Point: Each element of the sample space \(S\).
  • Event: Any subset of the sample space \(S\).
  • Elementary (Simple) Event: An event containing exactly one sample point.
  • Compound Event: An event containing more than one sample point.
  • Occurrence of an Event: An event \(E\) occurs if any one of its elementary events is the outcome.
  • Certain (Sure) Event: An event that must occur whatever the outcome — i.e. \(S\) itself. \(P(\text{sure event}) = 1\).
  • Impossible Event: An event that can never occur — i.e. \(\phi\). \(P(\phi) = 0\).
  • Favourable Outcomes: For event \(E \subset S\), the elementary events belonging to \(E\).
  • Equally Likely Outcomes: Each outcome is as likely to occur as any other.
Algebra of Events
  • Not A (Complement): \(A' = S - A\)
  • A or B: \(A \cup B\)
  • A and B: \(A \cap B\)
  • A but not B: \(A - B = A \cap B'\)

Classical & Axiomatic Probability

Theoretical (Classical) Approach

If there are \(n\) equally likely outcomes and \(m\) are favourable to event \(A\):

\[P(A) = \frac{m}{n} = \frac{\text{Number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}\]

Valid only when outcomes are equally likely and the total number is known.

Axiomatic Approach

For sample space \(S = \{w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n\}\):

  • \(0 \le P(w_i) \le 1\) for each \(w_i \in S\)
  • \(P(w_1) + P(w_2) + \cdots + P(w_n) = 1\)
  • For any event \(A\): \(P(A) = \displaystyle\sum_{w_i \in A} P(w_i)\)

Types of Events & Their Probabilities

Equally Likely Events

Events \(E\) and \(F\) are equally likely if \(P(E) = P(F)\).

Mutually Exclusive Events

Events are mutually exclusive if the occurrence of one prevents the occurrence of another:

\[A \cap B = \phi \implies P(A \cap B) = 0\]
Exhaustive Events

Events \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) are exhaustive if at least one of them must occur:

\[E_1 \cup E_2 \cup \cdots \cup E_n = S \implies P(E_1) + P(E_2) + \cdots + P(E_n) = 1\]

If \(E_i \cap E_j = \phi\) for \(i \ne j\) and \(\bigcup_{i=1}^n E_i = S\), the events are called mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

Independent Events

Events \(A\) and \(B\) are independent if the occurrence/non-occurrence of one does not affect the other:

\[P(A \cap B) = P(A)\cdot P(B)\]

If \(A\) and \(B\) are independent, then so are: \(A\) and \(B'\); \(A'\) and \(B\); \(A'\) and \(B'\).

Complementary Event

The complement of \(A\) is \(A' = \{w \in S : w \notin A\}\).

  • \(P(A) + P(A') = 1\)
  • \(P(A \cap A') = P(\phi) = 0\)
  • \(P(A \cup A') = P(S) = 1\)
  • \(P(A'') = P(A)\)

Results for Two Events \(A\) and \(B\)
  • \(P(A \cap B') = P(A) - P(A \cap B)\)
  • \(P(A' \cap B) = P(B) - P(A \cap B)\)
  • \(P(A' \cap B') = 1 - P(A \cup B)\)
  • \(P(A \cup B) = P(A \cap B') + P(A' \cap B) + P(A \cap B)\)
  • \(P(A) = P(A \cap B) + P(A \cap B')\)
  • \(P(B) = P(A \cap B) + P(A' \cap B)\)
  • Exactly one of \(A, B\) occurs: \[P(A \cap B') + P(A' \cap B) = P(A) + P(B) - 2P(A \cap B)\]
  • Neither \(A\) nor \(B\) occurs: \[P(A' \cap B') = 1 - P(A \cup B)\]
Results for Three Events \(A, B, C\)
  • Exactly one of \(A, B, C\) occurs: \[\begin{aligned} &P(A)+P(B)+P(C)\\ &\quad -2P(A\cap B)-2P(B\cap C)-2P(A\cap C)+3P(A\cap B\cap C) \end{aligned}\]
  • At least two of \(A, B, C\) occur: \[P(A\cap B)+P(B\cap C)+P(C\cap A)-2P(A\cap B\cap C)\]
  • Exactly two of \(A, B, C\) occur: \[P(A\cap B)+P(B\cap C)+P(A\cap C)-3P(A\cap B\cap C)\]
Monotonicity & Inequalities
  • If \(B \subseteq A\): \(P(A \cap B') = P(A) - P(B)\) and \(P(B) \le P(A)\)
  • \(P(A \cap B) \le P(A) \le P(A \cup B) \le P(A) + P(B)\)
  • Mutually exclusive events: \(P(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots \cup A_n) = \sum P(A_i)\)
  • Independent events — P(at least one occurs): \[P(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots \cup A_n) = 1 - P(A_1')P(A_2')\cdots P(A_n')\]
  • Bonferroni's Inequality: \[P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \cdots \cap A_n) \ge \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i) - (n-1)\]
  • Boole's Inequality: \[P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \cdots \cap A_n) \ge 1 - P(A_1') - P(A_2') - \cdots - P(A_n')\]

Odds, Conditional Probability & Multiplication Theorem

Odds in Favour and Against
  • Odds in favour of \(E\): \(\dfrac{P(E)}{P(E')}\)
  • Odds against \(E\): \(\dfrac{P(E')}{P(E)}\)
  • If odds in favour are \(a : b\), then \(P(E) = \dfrac{a}{a+b}\) and \(P(E') = \dfrac{b}{a+b}\).
Conditional Probability

The probability of event \(A\) given that \(B\) has already occurred \((P(B) \ne 0)\):

\[P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}\]

Similarly: \(P(B|A) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)},\quad P(A) \ne 0\)

Note: If \(A\) and \(B\) are independent, then \(P(A|B) = P(A)\) and \(P(B|A) = P(B)\).

Properties of Conditional Probability:

  • \(P(A'|B) + P(A|B) = 1\)
  • \(P((A \cup B)|F) = P(A|F) + P(B|F) - P((A \cap B)|F)\)
Multiplication Theorem

For two events:

\[P(A \cap B) = P(A)\cdot P(B|A) = P(B)\cdot P(A|B)\]

For \(n\) events:

\[\begin{aligned} P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \cdots \cap A_n) = &\;P(A_1)\cdot P(A_2|A_1)\cdot P(A_3|A_1\cap A_2)\\ &\cdots P(A_n|A_1\cap A_2\cap\cdots\cap A_{n-1}) \end{aligned}\]

Partition, Total Probability & Bayes' Theorem

Partition of a Sample Space

Events \(A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n\) form a partition of \(S\) if:

  • \(A_i \cap A_j = \phi\) for \(i \ne j\) (pairwise disjoint)
  • \(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots \cup A_n = S\) (exhaustive)
  • \(P(A_i) > 0\) for all \(i = 1, 2, \ldots, n\)
Theorem of Total Probability

Let \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) be a partition of \(S\). If \(A\) is any event, then:

\[P(A) = \sum_{r=1}^{n} P(E_r)\cdot P(A|E_r)\] \[= P(E_1)P(A|E_1) + P(E_2)P(A|E_2) + \cdots + P(E_n)P(A|E_n)\]
Bayes' Theorem

Let \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) be a partition of \(S\) and \(A\) be any event. The probability of \(E_i\) given \(A\) has occurred:

\[P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(E_i)\cdot P(A|E_i)}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} P(E_i)\cdot P(A|E_i)}, \quad i = 1, 2, \ldots, n\]
  • \(P(E_i)\) is called the priori probability (known before the experiment).
  • \(P(E_i|A)\) is called the posteriori probability (updated after observing \(A\)).

Random Variable & Probability Distribution

A random variable is a real-valued function \(X : S \to \mathbb{R}\) defined on the sample space \(S\).

  • Discrete Random Variable: Range is finite or countably infinite (e.g. number of heads in coin tosses).
  • Continuous Random Variable: Range is an interval \((a, b) \subseteq \mathbb{R}\).
Probability Distribution

If \(X\) takes values \(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\) with probabilities \(p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n\):

\(X\)\(x_1\)\(x_2\)\(x_3\)\(\cdots\)\(x_n\)
\(P(X)\)\(p_1\)\(p_2\)\(p_3\)\(\cdots\)\(p_n\)

with \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^n p_i = 1\) and \(0 \le p_i \le 1\).

Mean, Variance & Standard Deviation

Mean (Mathematical Expectation):

\[\mu = E(X) = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i x_i\]

Variance:

\[\sigma^2 = V(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2 \quad\text{where } E(X^2) = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\,p_i\]

Standard Deviation:

\[\sigma = \sqrt{V(X)} = \sqrt{E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2}\]
Important Results on Transformations

If \(Y = aX + b\):

  • \(E(Y) = aE(X) + b\)
  • \(V(Y) = a^2 V(X)\)
  • \(\sigma_Y = |a|\,\sigma_X\)

If \(Y = aX^2 + bX + c\):

\[E(Y) = aE(X^2) + bE(X) + c\]

Bernoulli Trials & Binomial Distribution

Trials are Bernoulli trials if:

  • Number of trials is finite.
  • Trials are independent.
  • Each trial has exactly two outcomes: success or failure.
  • Probability of success remains the same in each trial.
Binomial Distribution \(B(n, p)\)

Probability of exactly \(r\) successes in \(n\) Bernoulli trials:

\[P(X = r) = {}^nC_r\,p^r\,q^{n-r}, \quad r = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n\]

where \(p\) = probability of success, \(q = 1-p\) = probability of failure.

Geometrical Probability & Important Results

Geometrical Probability

When the total number of outcomes is infinite:

\[P = \frac{\text{Measure of region occupied by the event}}{\text{Measure of the whole region}}\]

where measure means length (1D), area (2D), or volume (3D).

Standard Results to Remember

Two dice — number of ways of getting total \(r\):

  • \((r - 1)\) ways, if \(2 \le r \le 7\)
  • \((13 - r)\) ways, if \(8 \le r \le 12\)

Playing Cards (pack of 52):

  • 4 suits (spades, hearts, diamonds, clubs), 13 cards each.
  • Face cards: King, Queen, Jack — total 12 face cards.
  • Honour cards: Ace, King, Queen, Jack — total 16 (4 per suit).
  • Red cards (hearts + diamonds): 26  |  Black cards (clubs + spades): 26.

\(n\) letters in \(n\) addressed envelopes:

  • P(all in right envelopes) \(= \dfrac{1}{n!}\)
  • P(at least one in wrong envelope) \(= 1 - \dfrac{1}{n!}\)
  • P(all in wrong envelopes — derangement) \(= \dfrac{1}{2!} - \dfrac{1}{3!} + \cdots + \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n!}\)
  • P(exactly \(r\) in right envelopes) \(= \dfrac{1}{r!}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2!}-\dfrac{1}{3!}+\cdots+\dfrac{(-1)^{n-r}}{(n-r)!}\right)\)

Selection of shoes from \(n\) pairs (\(k\) selected):

  • P(no complete pair) \(= \dfrac{2^k\cdot{}^nC_k}{{}^{2n}C_k}\)
  • P(at least one complete pair) \(= 1 - \dfrac{2^k\cdot{}^nC_k}{{}^{2n}C_k}\)

Mappings from set \(A\) (size \(m\)) to set \(B\) (size \(n\)), \(n \ge m\):

  • P(one-one function) \(= \dfrac{{}^nP_m}{n^m}\)
  • P(many-one function) \(= 1 - \dfrac{{}^nP_m}{n^m}\)
  • P(constant function) \(= \dfrac{n}{n^m}\)
  • P(bijective function) \(= \dfrac{n!}{n^n}\) (when \(m = n\))
