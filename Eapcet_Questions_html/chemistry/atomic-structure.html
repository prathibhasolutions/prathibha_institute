Atomic Structure

Atom is the smallest particle of matter which retains chemical properties of an element. Atomic radius is approximately \(10^{-8}\) cm.

An atom consists of three fundamental particles:

  • Electron
  • Proton
  • Neutron

Thomson Atomic Model

Atom is a positively charged sphere in which electrons are embedded.

This model is known as the Plum Pudding Model.

Rutherford Nuclear Model
  • Most of the atom is empty space.
  • Positive charge concentrated in nucleus.
  • Electrons revolve around nucleus.
  • Nuclear radius ≈ \(10^{-13}\) cm.
Atomic Number and Mass Number

\[ Z = \text{Number of protons} \]

  • \(Z\) = Atomic number
  • Equal to number of protons
  • Equal to number of electrons in neutral atom

\[ A = p + n \]

  • \(A\) = Mass number
  • \(p\) = Number of protons
  • \(n\) = Number of neutrons
Important Atomic Species
  • Isotopes: Same atomic number but different mass number.
  • Isobars: Same mass number but different atomic number.
  • Isotones: Same number of neutrons.
  • Isoelectronic species: Same number of electrons.
Electromagnetic Waves

\[ c = \nu \lambda \]

  • \(c\) = speed of light
  • \(\nu\) = frequency
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength
Planck Quantum Theory

\[ KE = h\nu - h\nu_0 \]

  • \(KE\) = Maximum kinetic energy of emitted electron
  • \(h\) = Planck constant
  • \(\nu\) = Frequency of incident radiation
  • \(\nu_0\) = Threshold frequency
Photoelectric Effect

\[ KE = h\nu - h\nu_0 \]

Threshold frequency = \(ν_0\)

Bohr's Atomic Model

\[ mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \]

  • \(m\) = Mass of electron
  • \(v\) = Velocity of electron
  • \(r\) = Radius of orbit
  • \(n\) = Principal quantum number
  • \(h\) = Planck constant

\[ \Delta E = h\nu \]

  • \(\Delta E\) = Energy difference between two energy levels
  • \(h\) = Planck constant
  • \(\nu\) = Frequency of emitted radiation
Radius, Velocity and Energy of Electron

\[ r_n = 0.53\frac{n^2}{Z} \, Å \]

  • \(r_n\) = Radius of nth orbit
  • \(n\) = Principal quantum number
  • \(Z\) = Atomic number

\[ v_n = 2.18\times10^6\frac{Z}{n} \, m/s \]

  • \(v_n\) = Velocity of electron
  • \(Z\) = Atomic number
  • \(n\) = Principal quantum number

\[ E_n = -13.6\frac{Z^2}{n^2} \, eV \]

  • \(E_n\) = Energy of electron in nth orbit
  • \(Z\) = Atomic number
  • \(n\) = Principal quantum number
Hydrogen Spectrum Series

Hydrogen Spectrum Formula

\[ \frac{1}{\lambda} = R \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right) \]

  • \(\lambda\) = Wavelength of spectral line
  • \(R\) = Rydberg constant \(1.097 \times 10^7 m^{-1}\)
  • \(n_1\) = Lower energy level
  • \(n_2\) = Higher energy level

de Broglie Hypothesis

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \]

  • \(\lambda\) = de Broglie wavelength
  • \(h\) = Planck constant
  • \(m\) = Mass of particle
  • \(v\) = Velocity of particle
Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

\[ \Delta x \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \]

Nodes in Orbitals

Node is region where probability of finding electron is zero.

  • Radial nodes = \(n-l-1\)
  • Angular nodes = \(l\)
  • Total nodes = \(n-1\)
Electronic Configuration

Order of orbital filling:

1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s < 5f < 6d < 7p

Important Principles
Aufbau Principle

Electrons occupy lowest energy orbitals first.

Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons have same four quantum numbers.

Hund Rule

Electrons occupy orbitals singly before pairing.
