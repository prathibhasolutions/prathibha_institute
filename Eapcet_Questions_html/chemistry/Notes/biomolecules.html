🌿 Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones or compounds that yield these on hydrolysis. General formula: Cx(H₂O)y. Also called saccharides (Latin saccharum = sugar).

Classification
TypeDescriptionExamples
MonosaccharidesCannot be hydrolysed furtherGlucose, Fructose, Galactose, Ribose
Oligosaccharides2–9 monosaccharide units on hydrolysisSucrose, Maltose, Lactose (disaccharides); Raffinose (trisaccharide)
PolysaccharidesPolymers of monosaccharides; non-sweet, insoluble (non-sugars)Starch, Cellulose, Glycogen
All naturally occurring monosaccharides belong to D-series. Killiani synthesis converts an aldose into next higher aldose.
Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — Dextrose

Found in grape sugar, corn sugar, blood sugar. Aldohexose.

Manufacture: (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁) + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + C₆H₁₂O₆ (sucrose hydrolysis) (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ + nH₂O → nC₆H₁₂O₆ (starch hydrolysis, H⁺/393K, 2–3 bar) α-D-glucose: specific rotation = +111.5° β-D-glucose: specific rotation = +19.5° Equilibrium mixture (mutarotation): +52.5° Anomeric carbon = C1 (hemiacetylic carbon)
Mutarotation: Spontaneous change in optical rotation of α or β form until equilibrium (+52.5°) is reached in water.

🧬 Amino Acids & Proteins
Amino Acids

Contain both —NH₂ and —COOH groups. General formula: H₂N—CHR—COOH (α-amino acid).

Classification by position of —NH₂: α-amino acid: —NH₂ on α-carbon β-amino acid: —NH₂ on β-carbon γ-amino acid: —NH₂ on γ-carbon Classification by side chain: Neutral: one —NH₂ + one —COOH (e.g. glycine, alanine) Acidic: one —NH₂ + two —COOH (e.g. aspartic acid) Basic: two+ —NH₂ + one —COOH (e.g. lysine) Except glycine, all α-amino acids are optically active (L-form natural) L-amino acids: —NH₂ on left in Fischer projection D-amino acids: found in some antibiotics/bacterial cell walls
Zwitter ion (isoelectric point): H₃N⁺—CHR—COO⁻
At low pH (acidic): exists as cation (H₃N⁺—CHR—COOH)
At high pH (basic): exists as anion (H₂N—CHR—COO⁻)

Peptides & Proteins
Peptide bond: —CO—NH— (amide linkage) Dipeptide: 1 peptide bond Tripeptide: 2 peptide bonds n amino acids → polypeptide with (n−1) peptide bonds Polypeptide mol. mass > 10,000 = Protein Two different amino acids → 2 dipeptides possible Three different amino acids → 6 tripeptides possible
Protein Structure: 1° (Primary): sequence of amino acids 2° (Secondary): α-helix (H-bonds) or β-pleated sheet 3° (Tertiary): 3D folding; stabilised by covalent, ionic, H-bonds, disulphide bonds 4° (Quaternary): arrangement of multiple chains Haemoglobin: globular protein; heme = prosthetic group; 4 polypeptide chains (2α × 141 + 2β × 146 = 574 aa) Sickle cell anaemia: glutamic acid → valine substitution
Classification of Proteins

By shape:

  • Fibrous proteins: polypeptide chains run parallel; insoluble; e.g. keratin (hair, wool), myosin, collagen
  • Globular proteins: chains coil spherically; soluble; e.g. insulin, albumin, haemoglobin

Tests for Proteins & Denaturation
Biuret Test: Protein + NaOH + dil. CuSO₄ → pink/violet colour (Detects peptide bonds — ≥2 peptide bonds needed) Millon's Test: Protein + Millon's reagent → red colour (Detects tyrosine; Millon's = Hg(NO₃)₂ + HNO₃) Xanthoprotein Test: Protein + conc. HNO₃ → yellow → NaOH → orange (Detects aromatic amino acids: Phe, Tyr, Trp) Iodine Reaction: Protein + I₂/KI → yellow colour
Denaturation: change in 3D structure of native protein (not 1° structure). Caused by: change in pH, heat, electrolytes, organic solvents. Examples: boiling egg, curdling milk, coagulation.
⚡ Enzymes

Enzymes are complex proteinoid organic compounds (biocatalysts) produced by living organisms. Mechanism: Lock and Key model.

Characteristics
  • Increase reaction rate up to 10⁶ to 10⁷ times.
  • Highly specific (urease hydrolyses urea only, not methyl urea).
  • Optimum temperature: 20–30°C.
  • Optimum pH: ~7 (pepsin: 1.8–2.2; trypsin: 7.5–8.3).
  • Very small amounts sufficient.
  • Endoenzymes: act inside cell; exoenzymes: act outside.
  • Named with suffix -ase after substrate: urease, maltase, diastase.
Mechanism: Enzyme + Substrate → [Enzyme-Substrate complex] → Product + Enzyme Co-enzymes (non-protein enhancers): Metal ions: Mn²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺, Na⁺, Zn²⁺, Co²⁺ Vitamins: thiamine, riboflavin, etc.

🧬 Nucleic Acids (DNA & RNA)
Key Terms
Nitrogenous Bases: Purines (double ring): Adenine (A), Guanine (G) Pyrimidines (single ring): Thymine (T), Uracil (U), Cytosine (C) Base pairing (H-bonds): A = T (2 H-bonds) [in DNA] A = U (2 H-bonds) [in RNA] C ≡ G (3 H-bonds) Pentose sugar: Ribose (in RNA) Deoxyribose (no —OH at C2) (in DNA) Nucleoside = Pentose + Nitrogenous base Nucleotide = Nucleoside + Phosphate group(s) Nucleic acid = Polymer of nucleotides

Structure of DNA & Genetic Code
  • Two polynucleotide chains, right-handed double helix.
  • 10 base pairs per turn of helix.
  • Two chains are antiparallel (5′→3′ and 3′→5′).
  • Held by H-bonds between complementary bases.
  • Heterocyclic base at C1, phosphate at C5′ of sugar.
Gene: portion of DNA coding for a specific protein Replication: DNA duplicating itself (parent strand = template) Genetic code: relation between amino acid and nucleotide triplet Codon: base triplet in m-RNA coding for one amino acid Anticodon: complementary triplet in t-RNA Code → DNA; Codon → m-RNA; Anticodon → t-RNA
🧈 Lipids

Constituents of animals and plants soluble in organic solvents (ether, CHCl₃, CCl₄) but insoluble in water. (Greek lipose = fat)

Fats vs Oils & Important Values
Fats: solid/semisolid above 20°C; higher saturated fatty acids Oils: liquid at ordinary temperature; lower or unsaturated fatty acids Both act as energy reservoirs (9 kcal/g) Common saturated fatty acids CH₃(CH₂)ₙCOOH: n=4: caproic; n=6: caprylic; n=8: capric n=10: lauric; n=12: myristic; n=14: palmitic; n=16: stearic Common unsaturated: oleic acid C₁₇H₃₃COOH; linoleic acid
Acid value: mg KOH to neutralise free acid in 1 g fat.
Saponification value: mg KOH to saponify 1 g fat.
Iodine value: g I₂ absorbed by 100 g fat (measures unsaturation).
R/M value: cc N/10 KOH to neutralise distillate of 5 g hydrolysed fat.
