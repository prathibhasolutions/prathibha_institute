Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Properties

Classification of elements helps in understanding similarities in properties and predicting behaviour of unknown elements.

Modern Periodic Table
Structural Features of Long Form Periodic Table
  • The periodic table consists of 7 periods and 18 groups.
  • Elements in the same group show similar properties.
  • Elements are classified into s, p, d, and f blocks.

Important Groups

  • Group 1 → Alkali metals
  • Group 2 → Alkaline earth metals
  • Group 16 → Chalcogens (ore forming elements)
  • Group 17 → Halogens (salt forming elements)
  • Group 18 → Noble gases
Anomalous Behaviour of First Element of a Group

The first element of each group differs from the rest of the elements in the group.

Reasons

  • Small size
  • High electronegativity
  • Absence of d orbitals

This behaviour is observed in second period elements (Li to F).

Predicting the Position of an Element

Period Number

  • Equal to highest principal quantum number (n).

Group Number

  • s-block → group = valence electrons
  • p-block → group = 10 + valence electrons
  • d-block → determined by (n−1)d and ns electrons
General Periodic Trends
Types of Atomic Radius
  • Covalent Radius – Half the distance between nuclei of two bonded atoms.
  • Metallic Radius – Distance between nuclei of adjacent metal atoms.
  • Ionic Radius – Radius of ions in ionic compounds.
Atomic Radius Trend
  • Decreases across a period.
  • Increases down a group.

Exceptions

  • Be > B
  • N > O
Ionisation Enthalpy (IE)

\[ M(g) \rightarrow M^+(g) + e^- \]

  • Increases across period
  • Decreases down group

Exceptions

  • Be > B
  • N > O
Electron Gain Enthalpy (Δeg)

\[ X + e^- \rightarrow X^- \]

  • Becomes more negative across period
  • Becomes less negative down group

Exception

  • Cl has greater electron affinity than F.
Electronegativity
  • Increases across period
  • Decreases down group
Valency
  • Valency increases from 1 to 4 across a period.
  • Then decreases from 4 to 0.
Chemical Reactivity
  • Metallic reactivity increases down group.
  • Non-metallic reactivity increases across period.
Melting and Boiling Points
  • Generally increase towards middle of period.
  • Decrease towards noble gases.
Electropositivity (Metallic Character)
  • Increases down group.
  • Decreases across period.
