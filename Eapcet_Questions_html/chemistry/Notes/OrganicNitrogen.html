Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen

Amines · Diazonium Salts · Cyanides · Nitro Compounds

🧪 Amines — Introduction & Classification

Amines are derived from NH₃ by replacing one or more H atoms with alkyl/aryl groups. The nitrogen atom is sp³-hybridised → pyramidal geometry.

Classification
1° Amine: R—NH₂ (one alkyl/aryl on N) 2° Amine: R—NH—R' (two groups on N) 3° Amine: R—N—R'R'' (three groups on N) Quaternary salt: R₄N⁺X⁻ Aliphatic: CH₃NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH Aromatic: C₆H₅NH₂ (aniline/benzenamine)
IUPAC: named as alkanamines e.g. CH₃CH₂NH₂ = ethanamine CH₃CH₂N(C₂H₅)₂ = N,N-diethylethanamine
Physical Properties
  • Lower aliphatic amines: gases with fishy smell.
  • C₃+ primary amines: liquids; higher members: solids.
  • Water solubility decreases as alkyl chain length increases.
  • Boiling point order: 1° > 2° > 3° (3° has no H for H-bonding).
  • Aromatic amines: colourless but darken on exposure to air.
⚙️ Methods of Preparation of Amines
(i) Reduction of Nitro Compounds
R—NO₂ → R—NH₂ (+ H₂O) Reagents: Sn/HCl, Ni/H₂, Zn/NaOH, Pd/H₂ Neutral medium (Zn/NH₄Cl) → hydroxylamine
(ii) Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides
R—X + NH₃ → RNH₂ + HX (1°) → R₂NH (2°) → R₃N (3°) → R₄N⁺X⁻ (quat.) Reactivity: RI > RBr > RCl Use excess NH₃ for major 1° amine product. Disadvantage: mixture of amines formed.
(iii) Reduction of Nitriles
R—C≡N → R—CH₂—NH₂ Reagents: Na(Hg)/C₂H₅OH or Ni/H₂
(iv) Schmidt Reaction
R—COOH + HN₃ → R—NH₂ + N₂ + CO₂ (Conc. H₂SO₄) Modification of Curtius degradation
(v) Reduction of Amides
R—CO—NH₂ → R—CH₂—NH₂ Reagent: LiAlH₄ (i), then H₂O (ii)
(vi) Gabriel's Phthalimide Reaction (1° only)
Phthalimide + KOH → Potassium phthalimide + R—X → N-alkyl phthalimide + KOH (aq) → 1° amine + potassium phthalate Only 1° amines; not for aryl amines (ArX unreactive)
(vii) Hofmann Bromamide Degradation
R—CO—NH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → R—NH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O Product amine has ONE LESS carbon than amide.
🧰 Chemical Properties of Amines
Basic Strength ★★★

Higher Kb → stronger base. Lower pKb → stronger base.

Order of basicity (aqueous): (C₂H₅)₂NH > (C₂H₅)₃N > C₂H₅NH₂ > NH₃ (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ Aliphatic amines > NH₃ > Aromatic amines (due to +I effect of alkyl vs resonance in aryl) Electron-donating groups (CH₃, OCH₃, NH₂) on ring → increase basicity of arylamines Electron-withdrawing groups (NO₂, X, CN) → decrease basicity of arylamines Ortho-effect: o-substituted arylamines are weaker bases than aniline (steric + electronic)
Key Chemical Reactions
(i) Alkylation (with RX → quaternary ammonium salt) (ii) Acylation: R—NH₂ + CH₃COCl → R—NH—CO—CH₃ + HCl (iii) Benzoylation (Schotten-Baumann): Aniline + C₆H₅COCl → benzanilide + HCl (iv) Carbylamine (only 1° amines): R—NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → R—N≡C + 3KCl + 3H₂O (isocyanide: foul smell — identification test for 1° amines) (v) Hinsberg reagent (C₆H₅SO₂Cl): 1°: forms sulphonamide soluble in alkali 2°: forms sulphonamide INsoluble in alkali 3°: no reaction (vi) Reaction with aldehydes: Ar—NH₂ + C₆H₅CHO → Ar—N=CH—C₆H₅ + H₂O (Schiff base formation)
Reaction with Nitrous Acid (HNO₂) ★★★
1° aliphatic amines: R—NH₂ + HNO₂ → R—N₂⁺Cl⁻ → R—OH + N₂↑ + HCl (unstable diazonium; N₂ evolution used to estimate amino acids/proteins) 1° aromatic amines (0–5°C): C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O (stable benzene diazonium chloride — DIAZOTISATION) 2° amines: form N-nitrosamines (R₂N—NO, yellow oil) 3° amines: form nitroso salts / no N₂ evolution
Electrophilic Substitution Reactions of Aniline

—NH₂ is a powerful ortho/para director due to electron donation by lone pair into ring.

(a) Bromination: C₆H₅NH₂ + 3Br₂(aq) → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (ppt) + 3HBr (b) Nitration: must protect —NH₂ by acetylation first (to avoid oxidation) Aniline → acetanilide → nitration gives p-nitroacetanilide (major) → hydrolysis → p-nitroaniline (c) Sulphonation: aniline + H₂SO₄ → sulphanilic acid (at 453–473 K) (zwitter ion) (d) No Friedel-Crafts reaction (forms salt with AlCl₃)

🧲 Cyanides (RCN) & Isocyanides (RNC)
Alkyl Cyanides (RCN — Nitriles)

IUPAC: alkanenitrile. e.g. C₃H₇CN = butanenitrile; C₆H₅CN = benzenenitrile

Preparation:
(i) R—X + KCN (alc.) → R—CN (major) + R—NC (minor) (ii) R—CO—NH₂ + P₂O₅ (heat) → R—C≡N + H₂O
Physical properties: Neutral, pleasant odour (bitter almonds); soluble in water and organics; poisonous. Chemical properties:
1. Hydrolysis: R—CN → R—CONH₂ (partial) → R—COOH + NH₃ (complete) 2. Reduction: R—CN + 4[H] → R—CH₂—NH₂ (Mendius reduction) (using Na/C₂H₅OH or LiAlH₄) 3. + Grignard reagent → ketone after hydrolysis
Alkyl Isocyanides (RNC — Isonitriles)

IUPAC: alkane isonitrile. e.g. CH₃NC = methyl isonitrile

Preparation:
(i) R—X + AgCN → R—NC (major) + R—CN (minor) (ii) Carbylamine: R—NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → R—NC + 3KCl + 3H₂O (iii) R—NH—CHO + POCl₃ → R—NC + H₂O (pyridine)
Physical properties: Colourless, unpleasant (foul) smell; soluble in organic solvents; insoluble in water. Chemical properties:
1. Hydrolysis: R—NC + H₂O → R—NH₂ + HCOOH 2. Reduction: R—NC + 4[H] → R—NH—CH₃ (2° amine) 3. Addition: R—NC + S → R—NCS (isothiocyanate) 4. Isomerisation at 250°C: R—NC → R—CN
🔵 Nitro Compounds

Hydrocarbon derivatives with —NO₂ group. IUPAC: nitroalkane.

Preparation
(i) R—X + AgNO₂ → R—NO₂ + AgX (ii) Nitration: Benzene + HNO₃(conc.)/H₂SO₄(conc.) → C₆H₅NO₂ (Nitrobenzene = oil of mirbane)
Physical Properties
  • Colourless, pleasant-smelling liquids.
  • B.P. much higher than isomeric nitriles.
  • Less soluble in water; soluble in organics.
