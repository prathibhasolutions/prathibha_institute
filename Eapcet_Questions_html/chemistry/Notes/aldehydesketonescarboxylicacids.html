1. Aldehydes & Ketones

1.1 Structure & Nomenclature

Aldehydes: Contain the –CHO group attached to an alkyl or aryl group; general formula: \( R-CHO \) or \( Ar-CHO \).

Ketones: Contain a carbonyl group \( C=O \) bonded to two carbon atoms; general formula: \( R-CO-R' \).

Nomenclature: For aldehydes, suffix -al is used (IUPAC). Ketones use suffix -one.

  • Common names based on alkyl substituents (e.g., acetone for propanone).
  • Aromatic aldehydes are named as derivatives of benzaldehyde (e.g., vanillin).

1.2 Preparation of Aldehydes & Ketones

From Alcohols (Oxidation)
  • 1° alcohols oxidized to aldehydes using PCC or controlled oxidation (prevent further oxidation to acid)
  • 2° alcohols oxidized to ketones using \( \mathrm{KMnO_4} \), \( \mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7} \)/\( \mathrm{H_2SO_4} \)
  • 3° alcohols do not oxidize easily
R–CH2OH + [O] → R–CHO (aldehyde)
R2CHOH + [O] → R2CO (ketone)
From Hydrocarbons
  • Oxymercuration of alkenes to form ketones and aldehydes
  • Ketel formation by oxidation of alkyl benzenes (side chain oxidation with \( \mathrm{KMnO_4} \))
From Grignard Reagents
  • Addition of Grignard reagents to nitriles → imines → hydrolysis → ketones
  • Addition to acid chlorides → ketones (dialkyl cadmium reactions as well)
Important Reactions & Special methods
  • Rosenmund Reduction: Acid chlorides reduced to aldehydes using \( \mathrm{H_2} \), palladium on BaSO4 poisoned catalyst.
  • Oxo Process: Hydroformylation of alkenes (alkene + CO + H2 with catalyst → aldehydes)
  • From acid chlorides & acid anhydrides: Partial reduction or hydrolysis
  • Dry distillation of calcium salts: Preparation of ketones and aldehydes from calcium salts
  • Formylation of aromatic rings: Gattermann-Koch reaction (with CO/HCl/AlCl3)

1.3 Physical Properties

  • Boiling points are higher than hydrocarbons but lower than alcohols due to no hydrogen bonding
  • Soluble in polar solvents; lower aldehydes and ketones soluble in water
  • Characteristic odors: aldehydes usually have pleasant odors, ketones sweet or minty

1.4 Chemical Properties & Important Reactions

A. Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

Due to partial positive charge on carbonyl carbon, nucleophiles attack producing addition products:

  • Addition of HCN → Cyanohydrins
  • Addition of NaHSO3 → bisulfite addition compounds (used for purification)
  • Addition of water → gem-diols (hydrate form); reversible
  • Addition of alcohols → hemiacetals and acetals (important in protection of carbonyl groups)
  • Addition of ammonia derivatives and hydrazines → imines, hydrazones, semicarbazones (used for identification)
\( \mathrm{RCHO + HCN \xrightarrow{KCN} RCH(OH)CN} \) (cyanohydrin)
B. Oxidation
  • Aldehydes easily oxidize to carboxylic acids with mild oxidants (Tollen's reagent, Fehling's, \( \mathrm{KMnO_4} \))
  • Ketones generally resistant to oxidation except with vigorous conditions
\( \mathrm{RCHO + [O] \rightarrow RCOOH} \)
C. Reduction
  • Aldehydes and ketones reduced to alcohols using LiAlH4 or NaBH4
  • Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reductions convert carbonyls fully to alkanes
\( \mathrm{RCHO + 2[H] \rightarrow RCH_2OH} \)
\( \mathrm{RCOR' + 2[H] \rightarrow RCH(OH)R'} \)
D. Aldol Condensation

A reaction of aldehydes or ketones with α-hydrogen under basic/acidic catalysis producing β-hydroxy aldehydes/ketones, which dehydrate to α,β-unsaturated compounds.

2 RCHO → RCH(OH)CH2CHO → RCH=CHCHO + H2O

Importance: C-C bond formation, formation of cinnamaldehyde, benzylideneacetone.

E. Cannizzaro Reaction

Disproportionation of non-enolizable aldehydes (no alpha H) in strong base yielding alcohol and carboxylic acid salts.

\( 2 RCHO + OH^- \rightarrow RCH_2OH + RCOO^- \)

Example: Benzaldehyde in \( \mathrm{NaOH} \) gives benzyl alcohol + sodium benzoate.

F. Haloform Reaction

Reaction of methyl ketones (or ethanol, acetaldehyde) with halogens \( \mathrm{X_2} \) and base to give haloform (CHX3) and carboxylate salt.

\( \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3 X_2 + 4 OH^- \rightarrow CHX_3 + CH_3COO^- + 3 H_2O} \)

Haloforms: Chloroform, bromoform, iodoform (yellow ppt).

G. Perkin Reaction

Condensation of aromatic aldehydes with anhydrides in presence of sodium salt of acid to give α,β-unsaturated acids.

Benzaldehyde + Acetic anhydride + Sodium acetate → Cinnamic acid + Acetic acid
H. Stephen Reduction

Reduction of nitriles to imines then hydrolysis to aldehydes using SnCl2 and HCl followed by water.

I. Identification Tests
  • Tollen’s Test: Silver mirror on aldehyde; ketones negative except α-hydroxyketones
  • Fehling’s Test: Brick red precipitate for aldehydes; ketones negative except α-hydroxyketones
  • Schiff’s Test: Pink color with aldehydes
  • Sodium bisulfite addition: Forms crystalline derivative; used for purification
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP): Formation of hydrazones, yellow or orange precipitates

2. Carboxylic Acids

2.1 Structure & Nomenclature

Compounds containing the carboxyl group (-COOH). General formula: \( R-COOH \).

Naming by replacing -e of alkane with -oic acid (IUPAC); example: ethanoic acid.

2.2 Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

By Oxidation
  • Primary alcohols or aldehydes with strong oxidizers (e.g. \( \mathrm{KMnO_4}, K_2Cr_2O_7/H_2SO_4 \))
  • Side chain oxidation of alkyl benzene gives benzoic acid
\( \mathrm{RCH_2OH \xrightarrow{[O]} RCOOH} \)
Other Methods
  • Hydrolysis of nitriles: \( \mathrm{R-C \equiv N + 2H_2O \rightarrow RCOOH + NH_3} \)
  • From Grignard reagents with dry ice (CO2): \( \mathrm{R-MgX + CO_2 \rightarrow RCOOMgX \xrightarrow{H^+} RCOOH} \)
  • Carbonation of Grignard reagents
  • Hydrolysis of acid halides and esters

2.3 Physical Properties

  • Higher boiling points due to dimer formation and hydrogen bonding
  • Soluble in water; solubility decreases with larger alkyl chain
  • Higher acidity than alcohols and phenols

2.4 Chemical Properties & Reactions

A. Acid-Base Properties
  • Carboxylic acids are weak acids, ionize to give carboxylate anions
  • Resonance stabilization of carboxylate ion increases acidity
  • Reactions with bases to form salts
\( \mathrm{RCOOH + NaOH \rightarrow RCOONa + H_2O} \)
B. Reduction
  • Reduced to primary alcohols by LiAlH4
\( \mathrm{RCOOH + 4[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} RCH_2OH + H_2O} \)
C. Reactions with PCl5, SOCl2

Formation of acid chlorides:

\( \mathrm{RCOOH + PCl_5 \rightarrow RCOCl + POCl_3 + HCl} \)
\( \mathrm{RCOOH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow RCOCl + SO_2 + HCl} \)
D. Esterification

Reaction with alcohols in presence of acid catalyst forming esters and water:

\( \mathrm{RCOOH + R'OH \xrightarrow{H^+} RCOOR' + H_2O} \)
E. Decarboxylation

Removal of carboxyl group as CO2, often using soda lime:

\( \mathrm{RCOOH \xrightarrow{NaOH+CaO, \Delta} RH + CO_2} \)
F. Halogenation

Alpha-halogenation of carboxylic acids (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction):

\( \mathrm{RCH_2COOH + Br_2 \xrightarrow{PCl_3} RCHBrCOOH + HBr} \)
G. Formation of Acid Derivatives

Different derivatives and reactions:

  • Acid chlorides: \( RCOCl \), highly reactive
  • Anhydrides: \( (RCO)_2O \)
  • Esters: \( RCOOR' \)
  • Amides: \( RCONH_2 \), prepared by reaction of acid chlorides with ammonia or amines

2.5 Laboratory Tests & Distinctions

  • Acid Test: Reaction with NaHCO3 to give CO2 effervescence (carboxylic acids positive)
  • Tollen’s and Fehling’s tests: Positive for aldehydes, negative for ketones & acids
  • Schotten-Baumann reaction: Acid chlorides reaction with amines to form amides
