Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

Aldehydes and Ketones

In aldehydes, the carbonyl group \( (C=O) \) is bonded to carbon and hydrogen, while in ketones, it is bonded to two carbon atoms.

Nature of Carbonyl Group

The carbon and oxygen of the carbonyl group are \( sp^2 \)-hybridised and the carbonyl double bond contains one σ-bond and one π-bond.

The electronegativity of oxygen is much higher than that of carbon, so the electron cloud is shifted towards the oxygen. Therefore, \( C-O \) bond is polar in nature.

Bond Angle: \( 120° \)

Polarity: \( C^{\delta+} - O^{\delta-} \)

Nomenclature

Nomenclature of Aldehydes

In IUPAC system, the suffix 'e' of alkane is replaced by the suffix 'al'.

Compound Common Name IUPAC Name
\( HCHO \) Formaldehyde Methanal
\( CH_3CHO \) Acetaldehyde Ethanal
Nomenclature of Ketones

In IUPAC system, the suffix 'e' of alkane is replaced by 'one'.

Compound Common Name IUPAC Name
\( CH_3COCH_3 \) Dimethyl ketone (Acetone) Propanone
\( CH_3COC_2H_5 \) Ethyl methyl ketone Butanone

Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

1. By Oxidation of Alcohols

Aldehydes and ketones are generally prepared by oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols, respectively.

Primary alcohol to aldehyde:

\[ RCH_2OH \xrightarrow{CrO_3} RCHO \]

Secondary alcohol to ketone:

\[ R-CH(OH)-R' \xrightarrow{CrO_3} R-CO-R' \]

2. By Dehydrogenation of Alcohols

In this method, alcohol vapours are passed over heavy metal catalysts (Ag or Cu). Primary and secondary alcohols give aldehydes and ketones.

Primary alcohol:

\[ R-CH_2-OH \xrightarrow{Cu/573K} RCHO + H_2 \]

Secondary alcohol:

\[ R-CH(OH)-R' \xrightarrow{Cu/573K} R-CO-R' + H_2 \]

3. By Ozonolysis of Alkenes

\[ R-CH=CH-R \xrightarrow[(ii)\ Zn/H_2O]{(i)\ O_3} 2RCHO \]

Example:

Cyclohexene gives benzaldehyde and cyclohexanone

4. By Hydration of Alkynes

Acetylene on hydration gives acetaldehyde and other alkynes on hydration give ketones.

Acetylene:

\[ CH \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4} CH_3CHO \]

Other alkynes:

\[ R-C \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4} R-CO-CH_3 \]

5. By Heating Ca Salt of Acid

To prepare ketone:

\[ (RCOO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-CO-R + CaCO_3 \]

To prepare aldehyde:

\[ (RCOO)_2Ca + (HCOO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2RCHO + 2CaCO_3 \]

6. By Decarboxylation and Dehydration of Aromatic Acids

To prepare benzaldehyde:

\[ C_6H_5COOH + HCOOH \xrightarrow{MnO/300°C} C_6H_5CHO + H_2O + CO_2 \]

To prepare acetophenone:

\[ C_6H_5COOH + CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{MnO/100°C} C_6H_5COCH_3 + H_2O + CO_2 \]

7. From gem-Dihalides

\[ CH_3CHCl_2 + 2KOH(aq) \xrightarrow{-2KCl} CH_3CH(OH)_2 \xrightarrow{-H_2O} CH_3CHO \]

1,1-dichloromethane (gem-dihalide)

Preparation of Aldehydes Only (Not Ketones)

1. Rosenmund Reduction

\[ C_6H_5COCl \xrightarrow{H_2/Pd-BaSO_4} C_6H_5CHO \]

Benzoyl chloride → Benzaldehyde

Note: Formaldehyde cannot be prepared by this method as \( HCOCl \) is highly unstable.

2. From Nitriles and Esters (Stephen Reaction)

Stephen reaction:

\[ RCN + SnCl_2 + HCl \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} RCH=NH \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} RCHO \]

DiBAL-H reduction:

\[ RCN \xrightarrow[(ii)\ H_2O]{(i)\ AlH(iBu)_2} RCHO \]

Similarly, esters can also be reduced to aldehydes with DiBAL-H:

\[ CH_3(CH_2)_9-CO-OC_2H_5 \xrightarrow[(ii)\ H_2O]{(i)\ DiBAL-H} CH_3(CH_2)_9-CHO \]

3. Etard Reaction

Toluene + \( CrO_2Cl_2 \) in \( CS_2 \) gives chromium complex which on hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde:

\[ C_6H_5CH_3 + CrO_2Cl_2 \xrightarrow{CS_2} \text{Complex} \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5CHO \]

4. Side Chain Halogenation Followed by Hydrolysis

\[ C_6H_5CH_3 \xrightarrow{Cl_2/h\nu} C_6H_5CHCl_2 \xrightarrow{H_2O/373K} C_6H_5CHO \]

5. Gattermann-Koch Synthesis

\[ C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{CO,\ HCl/Anhy.\ AlCl_3/CuCl} C_6H_5CHO \]

Preparation of Ketones Only (Not Aldehydes)

1. From Acyl Chlorides

\[ 2R-MgX + CdCl_2 \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} R_2Cd + 2MgXCl \]

\[ 2R'-COCl + R_2Cd \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} 2R'-CO-R + CdCl_2 \]

2. From Nitriles

\[ CH_3CH_2C \equiv N + C_6H_5MgBr \xrightarrow{ether} \text{Product} \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_2H_5-CO-C_6H_5 \]

(1-phenylpropanone)

3. Friedel-Crafts Acylation

\[ C_6H_6 + Ar/R-COCl \xrightarrow{Anhy.\ AlCl_3} C_6H_5-CO-Ar/R \]

4. Oppenauer Oxidation

\[ R_2CHOH + (CH_3)_2C=O \xrightarrow{[(CH_3)_3CO]_3Al} R_2C=O + (CH_3)_2CHOH \]

2° alcohol → ketone + isopropyl alcohol

Physical Properties of Aldehydes and Ketones

  • Methanal (HCHO) is a gas at room temperature. Its 40% aqueous solution is known as formalin. It is a reducing agent used in silvering of mirrors and decolourising vat dyes.
  • Ethanal (CH₃CHO) is a volatile liquid. Other aldehydes and ketones are liquid or solid at room temperature.
  • The boiling points of aldehydes and ketones are higher than hydrocarbons and ethers of comparable molecular mass due to high magnitude of dipole-dipole interactions.
  • Aldehydes and ketones have lower boiling point than alcohols of similar molecular masses due to absence of intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • The lower members of aldehydes and ketones are miscible with water due to the formation of hydrogen bond with water. However, solubility decreases with increase in length of alkyl chain.
  • Acetophenone is a hypnotic (sleep producing drug) so used as a medicine under the name hypnone.

Chemical Reactions of Aldehydes and Ketones

1. Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

General Mechanism:

\[ C=O + Nu^- \xrightarrow{Slow} C-O^- \xrightarrow{Fast} C-Nu \]

Reactivity Order:

\[ HCHO > CH_3CHO > C_2H_5CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > C_2H_5COCH_3 > C_2H_5COC_2H_5 > (CH_3)_3C-CO-C(CH_3)_3 \]

Reason: +I effect of alkyl groups decreases the positive charge on carbonyl carbon and steric hindrance (bulky alkyl groups hinder the approach of nucleophile).

(i) Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide (HCN)

\[ HCN + OH^- \rightleftharpoons CN^- + H_2O \]

\[ C=O + CN^- \rightarrow C(OH)(CN) \]

Product: Cyanohydrin

(ii) Addition of Sodium Hydrogen Sulphite

\[ C=O + NaHSO_3 \rightarrow C(OH)(SO_2Na) \]

White crystalline solid

Use: Separation and purification of aldehydes and ketones. The addition compound is water soluble and can be converted back to the original carbonyl compound by treating with dilute mineral acid or alkali.

(iii) Addition of Grignard Reagent

\[ C=O + R-MgX \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} C(OMgX)(R) \xrightarrow{H^+/H_2O} C(OH)(R) + Mg(OH)X \]

Types of alcohols formed:

  • Formaldehyde → 1° alcohols
  • Other aldehydes → 2° alcohols
  • Ketones → 3° alcohols
(iv) Addition of Lower Alcohols

Hemiacetal formation:

\[ R-CHO + R'OH \xrightarrow{HCl\ gas} R-CH(OH)(OR') \]

Acetal formation:

\[ R-CH(OH)(OR') + R'OH \xrightarrow{H^+} R-CH(OR')_2 + H_2O \]

Ketal formation:

\[ R_2C=O + \text{Ethylene glycol} \xrightarrow{dil.\ HCl} \text{Ethylene glycol ketal} + H_2O \]

(v) Addition of Ammonia and Its Derivatives

Formaldehyde:

\[ 6HCHO + 4NH_3 \rightarrow (CH_2)_6N_4 + 6H_2O \]

Hexamethylenetetramine (Urotropine)

Note: Urotropine on controlled nitration gives the well-known explosive RDX (Research and development explosive).

Acetaldehyde:

\[ CH_3CHO + NH_3 \rightarrow CH_3CH(OH)(NH_2) \xrightarrow{\Delta/-H_2O} CH_3CH=NH \]

Acetaldimine

Acetone:

\[ 2CH_3COCH_3 + NH_3 \rightarrow (CH_3)_2C(NH_2)CH_2COCH_3 \]

Diacetonamine

Schiff's base:

\[ RCHO + RNH_2 \rightarrow RCH=NR \]

2. Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination Reactions

General Reaction:

\[ C=O + H_2N-Z \rightarrow C(OH)(NHZ) \rightarrow C=N-Z + H_2O \]

where \( Z = \) alkyl, aryl, -OH, -NH₂, -C₆H₅NH, -NHCONH₂, etc.

N-Substituted Derivatives:
Z Group Reagent Name Product Name
-R Amine Substituted imine (Schiff's base)
-OH Hydroxylamine Oxime
-NH₂ Hydrazine Hydrazone
-NHC₆H₅ Phenylhydrazine Phenylhydrazone
-NH-C₆H₃(NO₂)₂ 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone
-NH-CO-NH₂ Semicarbazide Semicarbazone
3. Reduction

Aldehydes and ketones are reduced to primary and secondary alcohols respectively by sodium borohydride \( (NaBH_4) \) or lithium aluminium hydride \( [LiAlH_4] \).

Clemmensen Reduction:

\[ C=O \xrightarrow{Zn-Hg/HCl} CH_2 + H_2O \]

Wolff-Kishner Reduction:

\[ C=O \xrightarrow{H_2N-NH_2/-H_2O} C=N-NH_2 \xrightarrow{KOH/ethylene\ glycol/\Delta} CH_2 + N_2 \]

4. Oxidation

Aldehydes: Easily oxidised to carboxylic acids by \( HNO_3, KMnO_4, K_2Cr_2O_7 \), or even mild oxidising agents.

\[ RCHO \xrightarrow{[O]} RCOOH \]

Ketones: Oxidised under vigorous conditions (strong oxidising agents and elevated temperature).

\[ R-CH_2-CO-CH_2-R' \xrightarrow{[O]} R-COOH + R'CH_2COOH \]

Popoff's Rule: During oxidation of unsymmetrical ketones, the keto group stays preferentially with the smaller alkyl group.

Tests for Aldehydes:

(i) Tollen's Test: Aldehydes give bright silver mirror with Tollen's reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate).

\[ RCHO + 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ + 3OH^- \rightarrow RCOO^- + 2Ag \downarrow + 2H_2O + 4NH_3 \]

(ii) Fehling's Test: Fehling solution gives reddish brown precipitate with aldehydes (except benzaldehyde).

\[ R-CHO + 2Cu^{2+} + 5OH^- \rightarrow RCOO^- + Cu_2O \downarrow + 3H_2O \]

Fehling solution: Mixture of Fehling solution A (aqueous copper sulphate) and Fehling solution B (alkaline sodium potassium tartrate - Rochelle salt) in 1:1 ratio.

(iii) Benedict Solution: Aldehydes (except benzaldehyde) give red ppt. of \( Cu_2O \).

(iv) Schiff's Reagent: Aqueous solution of magenta or pink coloured rosaniline hydrochloride decolourised by passing \( SO_2 \). Aldehydes give pink colour but ketones do not.

5. Haloform Reaction

Aldehydes and ketones having at least one methyl group [3α-hydrogen] linked to the carbonyl carbon atom (methyl ketones) are oxidised by sodium hypohalite to sodium salts of corresponding carboxylic acids having one carbon atom less.

\[ R-CO-CH_3 \xrightarrow{NaOX} R-COO^-Na^+ + CHX_3 \downarrow \]

where \( X = Cl, Br, I \)

The methyl group is converted to haloform.
