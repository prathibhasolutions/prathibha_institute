Atomic Structure Notes
For EAPCET & JEE Mains

1. Subatomic Particles
  • Electron (\(e^-\)): Discovered by J.J. Thomson; charge \(= -1.602 \times 10^{-19}\) C, mass \(= 9.109 \times 10^{-31}\) kg
  • Proton (\(p^+\)): Discovered by E. Goldstein; charge \(= +1.602 \times 10^{-19}\) C, mass \(= 1.673 \times 10^{-27}\) kg
  • Neutron (\(n^0\)): Discovered by James Chadwick; charge = 0, mass \(= 1.675 \times 10^{-27}\) kg
Relative mass: Electron = 1/1836 u, Proton/neutron ≈ 1 u
2. Atomic Models
(a) Thomson's Model ("Plum Pudding"): Atom is a sphere of positive charge with embedded electrons.
(b) Rutherford’s Model:
  • Most of the atom is empty space.
  • Nucleus: Small, dense, positive center.
  • Electrons orbit around nucleus.
  • Fails to explain stability and line spectra.
(c) Bohr’s Model:
  • Electrons revolve in certain fixed orbits (energy levels).
  • Angular momentum quantized: \( m v r = n\hbar \) with \( n = 1, 2, 3, ... \)
  • Only orbits with integral multiple of \( h/2\pi \) are allowed.
  • No energy emission/absorption in same orbit; transition between levels emits/absorbs specific energy photons.
Radius of nth orbit: \( r_n = n^2 \frac{h^2}{4\pi^2 m_e Z e^2} \)
For hydrogen (\( Z=1 \)), \( r_n = 0.529 \,{\rm \AA} \times n^2 \)
Energy of nth orbit: \( E_n = -\frac{13.6\, \text{eV} \times Z^2}{n^2} \)
Wave number (Hydrogen spectrum, Balmer formula): \( \bar{v} = R Z^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) \)
where \( n_2>n_1, R = 1.097 \times 10^7 {\rm m}^{-1} \)
(d) Sommerfeld Model: Introduced elliptical orbits to explain fine-structure of spectral lines.
Limitations of Bohr's Model: Could not explain Zeeman Effect, fine spectra, multi-electron atoms.
3. Electromagnetic Radiation & Quantum Theory
  • Light shows both wave and particle nature (duality).
  • Velocity: \( c = 2.998 \times 10^8 \) m/s
  • Frequency (\(v\)), wavelength (\(\lambda\)): \( c = v \lambda \)
Energy of photon (Planck): \( E = h v = \frac{h c}{\lambda} \)
Photoelectric effect: \( h v = W + \frac{1}{2} m v^2 \)
where \( W \) = work function.
4. Wave Nature of Matter (de Broglie Hypothesis)
de Broglie wavelength: \( \lambda = \frac{h}{m v} \)
  • All moving particles have wave character; significant for low mass (electrons).
5. Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
\( \Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq \frac{h}{4\pi} \)
  • Both position (\(x\)) & momentum (\(p\)) cannot be precisely known simultaneously.
6. Quantum Mechanical Model
  • Based on Schrodinger's equation (\(\hat{H}\Psi = E\Psi\)).
  • \(\Psi\) = wave function, probability distribution of electron position.
  • Orbitals: Region in space where there is a high probability of finding electrons. Characterized by quantum numbers.
7. Quantum Numbers
  • Principal (\(n\)): Main energy level, n = 1, 2, ...
  • Azimuthal (\(l\)): sub-level (0 to n-1), s (0), p (1), d (2), f (3). Shapes: s-spherical, p-dumbbell.
  • Magnetic (\(m_l\)): Orientation, values \( -l \) to \( +l \)
  • Spin (\(m_s\)): +1/2 or -1/2, electron spin direction
No. of orbitals in n-th shell: \( n^2 \)
Max electrons in n-th shell: \( 2n^2 \)
8. Shapes of Orbitals & Nodes
s-orbital: Spherical
p-orbital: Dumbbell (3 orientations)
d-orbital: Cloverleaf (5 orientations)
No. of nodes:
Total nodes = n - 1
Radial nodes = n - l - 1
Angular nodes = l
9. Rules for Filling Electrons
Aufbau Principle: Lower energy orbitals fill first.
Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom have the same set of four quantum numbers.
Hund’s Rule: Every orbital in a sublevel is singly occupied before any is doubly occupied. Electrons remain unpaired as far as possible.
Order of filling (by energy):
\( 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s \)
10. Electronic Configuration
  • Short form: \( 1s^2 \ 2s^2 \ 2p^6 ...
  • Stability: Half-filled and fully filled subshells are unusually stable due to symmetric distribution & exchange energy.
11. Magnetic Properties
Paramagnetic: Unpaired electrons, attracted by magnetic field.
Diamagnetic: All electrons paired, weakly repelled.
Magnetic moment: \( \mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \ \text{BM} \)
\( n = \) number of unpaired electrons
12. Hydrogen Spectrum
  • Lyman series (UV): \( n_1=1 \), \( n_2=2,3,... \)
  • Balmer series (visible): \( n_1=2 \), \( n_2=3,4,... \)
  • Paschen, Brackett, Pfund (IR): \( n_1=3,4,5... \)
Rydberg formula: \( \bar{v} = R \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) \)
