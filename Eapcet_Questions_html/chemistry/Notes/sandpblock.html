S-Block & P-Block Elements

Groups 1–2 (s-Block) · Groups 13–18 (p-Block)

🟢 PART A — S-Block Elements (Chapter 18)

🧪 Group 1 — Alkali Metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr)

Last electron enters ns¹ orbital. Do not occur free in nature. Oxides/hydroxides are strongly alkaline.

General Characteristics
Electronic config: [noble gas] ns¹ All have bcc lattice (CN = 8) Trends (down the group): Atomic/ionic radii ↑ Ionisation enthalpy ↓ Electropositive character ↑ Softness ↑ Melting/boiling points ↓ Electrical conductivity ↑ Reducing character order: Na < K < Rb < Cs < Li (Li highest in aqueous solution — anomalous) Hydration enthalpy order: Li⁺ > Na⁺ > K⁺ > Rb⁺ > Cs⁺ (smallest size → highest hydration) Flame colours: Li: Crimson Red Na: Yellow K: Violet Rb: Red-violet Cs: Blue
Fr is a liquid at room temperature. Na density > K density (anomalous — K has abnormally large size increase). Li is lightest known metal. KO₂ (potassium superoxide) used in submarines/oxygen masks.
Chemical Reactions
Action of O₂: Li → Li₂O (monoxide) Na → Na₂O₂ (peroxide) [yellow, due to superoxide impurity] K, Rb, Cs → MO₂ (superoxide, coloured, paramagnetic) Action of water: 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂↑ Order: Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs (LiOH removes CO₂ in submarines) Action of H₂ (at 673 K): 2M + H₂ → 2M⁺H⁻ (ionic hydrides) With N₂: 6Li + N₂ → 2Li₃N (only Li forms nitride!) Nitrates: LiNO₃ → Li₂O + NO₂ + O₂ NaNO₃ → NaNO₂ + O₂ (other alkali metals form nitrites) Na₂SO₄·10H₂O = Glauber's salt Li₂SO₄ is insoluble (unlike rest)
Anomalous Behaviour of Li (Diagonal Relationship with Mg)
  • Smallest size, highest IE and electronegativity in group.
  • Forms monoxide (Li₂O), not peroxide/superoxide.
  • Li₂CO₃, LiF, Li₃PO₄ are insoluble (others soluble).
  • Li₂CO₃ decomposes on heating (others don't).
  • Only alkali metal reacting with N₂.
Li resembles Mg (diagonal): similar atomic radii (1.31 vs 1.34 Å), ionic radii (Li⁺=0.60, Mg²⁺=0.65 Å), electronegativity (1.0 vs 1.2), both form monoxides, both LiCl and MgCl₂ are covalent, both react with N₂.

💠 Group 2 — Alkaline Earth Metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra)

Valence shell: ns². Oxides alkaline; found in earth's crust. Mg in chlorophyll; Ca in bones.

General Characteristics
Oxidation state: always +2 Smaller and denser than alkali metals (same period) Be/Mg: hcp; Ca/Sr: ccp; Ba: bcc Flame colours: Ca: Brick red Sr: Crimson red Ba: Apple green Ra: Crimson (Be and Mg: no flame colour — high IE) Hydration: Ca²⁺ → Be²⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Sr²⁺ > Ba²⁺ Reactivity with water: Be < Mg < Ca < Sr < Ba (Mg(OH)₂ = milk of magnesia) (Ca(OH)₂ = lime water; Ba(OH)₂ = baryta)
Chemical Reactions
With O₂: Be, Mg, Ca → MO (monoxide) Sr, Ba, Ra → MO₂ (peroxide) With halogens: M + X₂ → MX₂ (all form halides) Solubility of halides: MgX² > CaX² > SrX² > BaX² BeX₂ and MgX₂: covalent; BeCl₂ is dimer With N₂: 3M + N₂ → M₃N₂ (hydrolysis → NH₃) With C: MC + 2C → MC₂ (ionic carbides → C₂H₂ with H₂O) Exception: Be₂C → gives CH₄ (like Li) Carbonates: M(HCO₃)₂ exist in solution only MCO₃ → MO + CO₂ (on heating, stability increases ↓ group) Bicarbonates → exist only in solution
Anomalous Behaviour of Be (Diagonal Relationship with Al)
  • Be is denser, harder than other group 2 metals. High m.p. (1551 K vs Mg 924 K).
  • BeO and Be(OH)₂ are amphoteric (others basic).
  • Be does NOT react with water even at high T.
  • Be₂C → gives CH₄ (not C₂H₂ like rest).
  • BeX₂ are covalent, hygroscopic, fume in air; BeCl₂ is a dimer.
Be resembles Al (diagonal): both stable in air, both don't decompose water at 373 K, both form fluoro complex anions (BeF₄²⁻ and AlF₆³⁻), both passive with conc. HNO₃, both react with NaOH releasing H₂, both have amphoteric oxides/hydroxides.

🟡 PART B — P-Block Elements (Chapter 19)

🟡 Group 13 — Boron Family (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl)

Valence config: ns²np¹. B is non-metal; rest are metals. Boron shows anomalous behaviour (smallest, highest IE, no d-orbitals, allotropy).

Key Properties
  • Oxidation state: +3 (except Tl which shows +1 more stable — inert pair effect). TlCl₃ decomposes to TlCl + Cl₂.
  • Oxides/hydroxides: B(OH)₃ = acidic; Al₂O₃ = amphoteric; Ga, In, Tl = basic (moving down).
  • All form M₂O₃ on heating with O₂ (except Tl → Tl₂O).
  • Halides: BX₃ = covalent monomers; AlX₃ = covalent dimers (Al₂X₆) except AlF₃ (ionic).
  • Lewis acidity of BX₃: BI₃ > BBr₃ > BCl₃ > BF₃ (back-bonding in BF₃).
Diborane (B₂H₆): sp³ hybridised B; 4 terminal B—H bonds (normal) 2 bridge B—H—B bonds (3c-2e bonds / banana bonds) Prepared: 4BF₃ + 3LiAlH₄ → 2B₂H₆ + 3LiF + 3AlF₃ Burns: B₂H₆ + 3O₂ → B₂O₃ + 3H₂O
Boron Compounds
Borax [Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O]: Aqueous solution is basic: Na₂B₄O₇ + 7H₂O → 2NaOH + 4H₃BO₃ Borax bead test: detects coloured metal ions (Co → blue, Fe → green, Ni → brown bead) Boric acid [H₃BO₃ or B(OH)₃]: Weak monobasic Lewis acid H₃BO₃ + 2H₂O → [B(OH)₄]⁻ + H₃O⁺ Heating: H₃BO₃ → HBO₂ → H₂B₄O₇ → B₂O₃ Uses: antiseptic/eye lotion (boric lotion), food preservative Borazine (B₃N₃H₆): Inorganic benzene; colourless liquid 6-membered ring of alternating B and N atoms More reactive than benzene (partial delocalisation)
Aluminium Compounds
AlCl₃ (anhydrous): Preparation: 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃ (NOT by heating AlCl₃·6H₂O → gives Al(OH)₃) Fumes in moist air (hydrolysis) Lewis acid; catalyst in Friedel-Crafts Al₂O₃ (Alumina): Corundum: colourless; Ruby (red, Cr³⁺); Sapphire (blue); Emerald (green)
Alum general formula: X₂SO₄·Y₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O
X = monovalent cation (K⁺, Na⁺, NH₄⁺)
Y = trivalent cation (Al³⁺, Cr³⁺, Fe³⁺)
Li⁺ does NOT form alum

Potash alum: K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O
🔷 Group 14 — Carbon Family (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb)

Valence config: ns²np². Oxidation states: +2 and +4. Pb shows +2 more stable (inert pair effect).

Carbon — Allotropes

Silicon Compounds
SiO₂: 3D covalent network; Si tetrahedral to 4 O SiO₂ + NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + H₂O SiO₂ + HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O (etching of glass) Silicones: (R₂SiO)ₙ repeating unit Chemically inert, water-repellent, heat-resistant, good electrical insulators; used as lubricants Silicates: basic unit = SiO₄⁴⁻ Orthosilicate (0 O shared): ZrSiO₄ Pyrosilicate (1 O shared): Si₂O₇⁶⁻ Zeolites (3D): used as catalyst (ZSM-5)
Glass types: Hard glass: K₂O·CaO·4SiO₂ (resistant to acid) Flint glass: K₂O·PbO·4SiO₂ (high refractive index) Pyrex: borosilicate (low thermal expansion) Crooke's glass: contains CeO₂ (absorbs UV) Quartz glass: pure silica (optical instruments) General formula: Na₂O·CaO·6SiO₂ Coloured glasses: CoO (blue), Fe²⁺/Cr (green), Fe³⁺ (yellow), Au (ruby/red)
🔴 Group 15 — Nitrogen Family (N, P, As, Sb, Bi)

Valence config: ns²np³. N & P: non-metals; As, Sb: metalloids; Bi: metal. Nitrogen = 78% of air.

Key Properties
Oxidation states: N (–3 to +5), P (–3, +3, +5), etc. Allotropy: N (α, β); P (white, red, black); As, Sb (multiple) N₂ is diatomic; others are tetraatomic E₄ N≡N very stable (bond energy = 946 kJ/mol) P₄ (white P) very reactive, poisonous, stored in water Red P: stable, non-poisonous, less reactive Black P: most stable allotrope of P
Nitrogen Compounds
NO: colourless gas; odd electrons; forms NO₂ on oxidation NO₂: brown/reddish gas; odd electrons → dimerises to N₂O₄ HNO₃ (King of chemicals / Aqua fortis) 3Fe + 4HNO₃(dil.) → 3FeO + 4NO + 4H₂O (brown ring test) 4HNO₃(conc.) → 4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O (dilute gives NO) Aqua regia: 3 parts HCl + 1 part HNO₃ (dissolves Au, Pt) N₂O = laughing gas Brown ring test for NO₃⁻: FeSO₄ + conc. H₂SO₄ + NO₃⁻ → brown ring
Phosphorus Compounds
PCl₃: pyramidal (sp³); P + Cl₂ (limited) → PCl₃; fumes in water → H₃PO₃ + HCl PCl₅: trigonal bipyramidal (sp³d); P + excess Cl₂ → PCl₅; in solution exists as PCl₄⁺ + PCl₆⁻ NH₄H₂PO₄ = match box head; P₄S₃ = match box side (red P) Oxoacids of Phosphorus: H₃PO₃ (phosphorous acid): 2 replaceable H, dibasic H₃PO₄ (orthophosphoric acid): 3 replaceable H, tribasic H₃PO₂ (hypophosphorous acid): 1 replaceable H, monobasic H₄P₂O₇ (pyrophosphoric acid): 4 replaceable H, tetrabasic
⚫ Group 16 — Chalcogens (O, S, Se, Te, Po)

Valence config: ns²np⁴. O₂ content of air = 21%. S is an important industrial element.

Ozone (O₃)
Structure: angular/bent; bond angle 116°48'; bond length 128 pm Preparation: electrical discharge through O₂ (1% O₃ formed) Bond energy = 284 kJ/mol Reactions (as oxidising agent): O₃ → O₂ + [O] (nascent oxygen) PbS + 4[O] → PbSO₄ 2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂ (I₂ turns starch blue — test for O₃) Uses: germicide/disinfectant, bleaching agent (oils, ivory, delicate fibres), detecting double bonds in unsaturated compounds
Sulphur & SO₂
Allotropes: Rhombic (α, stable at room T) ⇌ Monoclinic (β, stable above 369K) SO₂: colourless, pungent, soluble in water SO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂SO₃ (sulphurous acid) Turns lime water milky → CaSO₃; excess SO₂ → Ca(HSO₃)₂ (clears) Reducing agent (bleaches wool/silk) SO₂ + Cl₂ → SO₂Cl₂ (sulphuryl chloride) SO₂ + CO → COCl₂ (phosgene) Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O = Hypo (sodium thiosulphate) Used in photography (fixing), antichlor, iodometric titrations
Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄) — King of Chemicals
Manufacture: Contact process (1) S or sulphide ores + O₂ → SO₂ (2) 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ (V₂O₅ catalyst) (3) SO₃ absorbed in H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum) + H₂O → H₂SO₄ Properties: 1. Dense, colourless, oily liquid (conc. = 98%) 2. Strong dehydrating agent: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ → 12C + 11H₂O (with conc. H₂SO₄) 3. Hot conc.: strong oxidising agent: Cu + H₂SO₄(conc.) → CuSO₄ + SO₂↑ + H₂O 4. SO₄²⁻ ion is tetrahedral; S is sp³-hybridised Oxoacids of Sulphur: H₂SO₃: sulphurous acid; H₂SO₄: sulphuric acid H₂S₂O₇: oleum (pyrosulphuric acid) H₂S₂O₃: thiosulphuric acid; H₂S₂O₄: dithionous acid H₂SO₅: Caro's acid (peroxomonosulphuric acid) H₂S₂O₈: peroxodisulphuric acid
🟦 Group 17 — Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At)

Valence config: ns²np⁵. Salt-forming elements. Most electronegative: F. Strongest oxidising agent: F₂.

Physical Properties Trends
PropertyOrder

Bond energyCl₂ > Br₂ > F₂ > I₂ (F₂ weak due to lone pair repulsion)
Bond lengthF-F < Cl-Cl < Br-Br < I-I

I₂ + KI → KI₃ (soluble); I₂ + starch → blue complex (test for I₂).

Chlorine Chemistry ★★★
Preparation: Weldon: MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂ Deacon: 4HCl + O₂ → 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O (CuCl₂ catalyst, 400°C) Electrolysis of brine (Nelson cell) Key Reactions: Cl₂ + H₂O → HOCl + HCl (bleaching by oxidation; permanent) Cl₂ + NaOH(cold) → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O (Javelle water) NH₃(excess) + 3Cl₂ → N₂ + 6NH₄Cl (N₂ gas, no fumes) NH₃ + 3Cl₂(excess) → NCl₃ + 3HCl HCl + Na₂CO₃ → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑ HCl + NaHCO₃ → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑ Chromyl chloride test (for Cl⁻): Cl⁻ + K₂Cr₂O₇ + H₂SO₄ → CrO₂Cl₂ (red vapour) CrO₂Cl₂ + NaOH → Na₂CrO₄ (yellow)
Fluorine (Anomalous Behaviour) & Interhalogen Compounds
Why F is anomalous:
  • Smallest size; lowest F—F bond energy.
  • Only −1 oxidation state (no d-orbitals).
  • Strongest oxidising agent; most electronegative element.
  • Forms strong H-bonds (HF: highest BP in HX series).
  • Only 1 oxoacid: HOF (hypofluorous acid).
Interhalogen Compounds (AXₙ):
Types: XY, XY₃, XY₅, XY₇ (larger halogen = central atom) Reactivity: ClF₃ > BrF₃ > IF₃ > ClF > BrF > IF More reactive than halogens (except F₂) Hydrolysis: halide of smaller + oxohalide of larger halogen Oxoacid acidity: HOF > HOCl > HOBr > HOI Higher oxoacids of F do not exist (no d-orbitals)
⚪ Group 18 — Noble Gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn)

Valence config: ns²np⁶ (except He: 1s²). Isolated from air by Ramsay (1898). Monoatomic gases (γ = Cp/Cv = 1.67).

Physical Properties & Abundance
Abundance in air: Ar > Ne > He > Kr > Xe IE very high; decreases down group Electron affinity ≈ 0 Solubility in water increases He → Rn M.p., b.p., density increase down group Liquefication very difficult (weak van der Waals forces) Uses: He+O₂: deep sea divers' breathing gas; balloons (non-inflammable) Ne: discharge lamps; greenhouses (chlorophyll formation) Ar: filling incandescent bulbs; inert atmosphere in metallurgy Rn: cancer treatment
