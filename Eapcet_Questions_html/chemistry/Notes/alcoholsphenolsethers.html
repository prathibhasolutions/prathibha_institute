1. Alcohols
1.1. Introduction & Classification
Definition: Alcohols are hydroxy derivatives of aliphatic hydrocarbons where the –OH is attached to sp3 carbon.
Formula: CnH2n+1OH
Classification by:
  • Number of –OH Groups: Monohydric, Dihydric (Glycols), Trihydric (Glycerol)
  • Class: Primary (1°): –CH2OH; Secondary (2°): –CHOH–; Tertiary (3°): –C(OH)–
Nomenclature:
  • IUPAC: Longest chain with –OH, suffix "-ol"
  • Common Names: Alkyl alcohol (e.g. ethyl alcohol)
1.2. Methods of Preparation
  • From Alkenes
    • Acid-catalyzed hydration (Markovnikov rule):
    CH2=CH2 + H2O →(H+) CH3CH2OH
    (Carbocation rearrangement possible; gives 2°/3° alcohol if applicable)
    • Hydroboration-Oxidation (Anti-Markovnikov):
    CH2=CH2 + BH3→ [trialkylborane] →(H2O2/OH⁻) CH3CH2OH
  • From Carbonyl Compounds (Reduction)
    • Aldehyde + NaBH4/LiAlH4 → 1° alcohol
    • Ketone + NaBH4/LiAlH4 → 2° alcohol
    RCHO + 2[H] → RCH2OH
  • By Grignard Reagent on Carbonyls:
    R–MgX + R'CHO → R'CH(OH)R (2° alcohol)
    R–MgX + HCHO → RCH2OH (1° alcohol)
    R–MgX + R'2CO → R'2C(OH)R (3° alcohol)
  • From Alkyl Halides (Nucleophilic Substitution):
    R–X + KOH(aq) → R–OH + KX
  • By Fermentation (Ethanol):
    C6H12O6 →(Yeast) 2C2H5OH + 2CO2
Reagent Selection Table:

1.3. Physical Properties
  • Boiling point: ↑ (due to strong H-bond, alcohol > ether > alkane)
  • Solubility: Decreases with ↑mol. mass; lower alcohols miscible with H2O
  • Density: Alcohols denser than hydrocarbons of same size
1.4. Chemical Reactions
A. Reactions of –OH group (Acidic character)
  • With sodium: 2R–OH + 2Na → 2R–O⁻Na⁺ + H2↑
  • With PCl5, PCl3, SOCl2: R–OH + PCl5 → R–Cl + POCl3 + HCl
  • With carboxylic acid (Esterification, Fischer-Speier): R–OH + R'COOH →(H+, Δ) R'COOR + H2O
  • Dehydration (intramolecular): R–CH2–CH2–OH →(conc H2SO4, 170°C) R–CH=CH2 + H2O
  • Oxidation: (see next nested card for details)
B. Oxidation Pathways
  • 1° Alcohol →(mild oxidant) Aldehyde →(strong oxidant) Acid
  • 2° Alcohol →(oxidant) Ketone →(strong/hot oxidant) Acid + Shorter Chain Compounds
  • 3° Alcohols → Resistance to oxidation
R–CH2OH + [O] → R–CHO → R–COOH R2CH–OH + [O] → R2CO
(Oxidants: KMnO4, K2Cr2O7/H2SO4, PCC, etc.)
C. Mechanism: Dehydration of Alcohols
2°/3° alcohols: E1 mechanism (carbocation intermediate, arrangement possible).
1° alcohols: E2 (concerted elimination, no rearrangement).
  • Step 1: Protonation of –OH
  • Step 2: Departure as H2O, forming carbocation (if stable)
  • Step 3: Loss of β-H+ to give alkene
Saytzeff's Rule: More substituted alkene is major.
D. Distinguishing 1°, 2°, 3° Alcohols (Lab Tests)
  • Lucas Test: ZnCl2/conc HCl + alcohol.
    3° alcohol – turbidity instantly; 2° – minutes; 1° – no/little turbidity.
  • Victor Meyer Test: Red (1°), Blue (2°), No colour (3°)
  • Oxidation Test: 1° gives acid, 2° gives ketone, 3° unchanged (resist oxidants).
E. Other Important Reactions
  • R–OH + R'–COOH (conc. H2SO4) → R'COOR + H2O(Esterification)
  • R–CH2OH + Na → R–CH2O⁻Na⁺ + ½H2
  • R–OH + SOCl2 → R–Cl + SO2 + HCl
  • Pinacol-Pinacolone Rearrangement (for 1,2-diols)
  • Pinacol (glycol) + H+ → Pinacolone + H2O
  • Oxidative cleavage of glycols with HIO4
1.5. Uses & Applications
  • Solvents (ethanol, methanol)
  • Starting materials for organic syntheses
  • Antifreeze (ethylene glycol)
  • Fuel (bioethanol)
  • Sterilizer/disinfectant
2. Phenols
2.1. Introduction, Structure, and Acidity
Definition: Hydroxy derivatives of benzene, –OH group directly bonded to aromatic ring.
Structure: C6H5OH
Acidity (trend):
  • Phenol > Alcohols (due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide anion)
  • Easily gives H+ with alkali and sodium
C6H5OH + Na → C6H5O⁻Na⁺ + ½H2↑
2.2. Methods of Preparation
  • From Chlorobenzene (Dow’s process): C6H5Cl + NaOH (623K, 300atm) → C6H5OH + NaCl
  • From Benzene Sulfonic Acid: C6H5SO3H + 2NaOH (fused) → C6H5ONa + H2O + Na2SO3 (acidify) → C6H5OH
  • From Diazonium Salts: C6H5N2Cl + H2O → C6H5OH + N2 + HCl
  • From Cumene: Cumene O2 (air oxidation) → Cumene hydroperoxide (acid) → Phenol + Acetone
2.3. Physical & Chemical Properties
Boiling Point: Very high (H-bonding, ortho effect).
Solubility: Moderately soluble in water, ↑ with temp (breaks at high conc.).
Acidity: Phenols more acidic than alcohols; acidity ↑ with electron withdrawing groups (E.g. –NO2 at ortho/para).
2.4. Chemical Reactions
A. Reactions Due to –OH (Acidic Nature)
  • C6H5OH + Na → C6H5ONa + ½H2
  • C6H5OH + NaOH → C6H5ONa + H2O
  • With FeCl3: Violet colour (test for phenolic –OH)
  • With Zn: Hexa phenolate of Zn
B. Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions
Phenol is highly reactive at o-/p-places due to +M effect of –OH.
  • Halogenation: C6H5OH + 3Br2(aq) → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt)
  • Nitration: C6H5OH + dil. HNO3 → o-& p-nitrophenol
    conc. HNO3: Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol)
  • Sulphonation: C6H5OH + H2SO4(dil.) → o-/p-phenolsulfonic acid
C. Kolbe’s Reaction
Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction: C6H5ONa + CO2 (6 atm, 125°C) → o-Hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid, Aspirin precursor)
D. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction
Formylation of phenols at ortho-position using CHCl3 + NaOH → Salicylaldehyde: C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3NaOH → o-HO–C6H4–CHO + 3NaCl + 2H2O
Mechanism: formation of dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) as electrophile.
E. Oxidation & Other Reactions
  • Oxidation: Phenol —[K2Cr2O7]→ p-benzoquinone
  • Reduction: Phenol —[Zn/H]→ Benzene
  • Esterification: C6H5OH + RCOCl → C6H5OCO–R + HCl
  • Formation of Azo Dye: Phenol + diazonium salt (NaNO2/HCl) → p-hydroxyazobenzene (bright colour)
F. Laboratory and Distinction Tests
  • FeCl3 test: Gives violet/purple colour with (most) phenols.
    Bromine water: White ppt indicates 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
    Litmus Test: Turns blue litmus red (weak acid).
  • Compound Distinction: Phenol does not reduce Tollens'/Fehling's reagent, unlike aldehydes; reacts with NaOH, not carboxylic acids.
G. Special Named Reactions with Phenols
  • Fries Rearrangement: Phenyl ester + AlCl3 → o-& p-hydroxy ketones (acyl phenols)
  • Claisen Rearrangement (allyl phenyl ether only): Allyl phenyl ether →(Δ) o-allyl phenol
  • Phthalein Reaction: Phenol + phthalic anhydride + H2SO4 → Phenolphthalein (indicator)
2.5. Applications & Uses
  • Antiseptics (Dettol contains chloroxylenol + phenol)
  • Disinfectants, dyes (azo dye synthesis)
  • Pesticides, resins, plastics, aspirin precursor
3. Ethers
3.1. Introduction, Structure, Nomenclature
Definition: Compounds with general formula R–O–R', oxygen linked to two carbons.
Types: Simple (R = R'); Mixed (R ≠ R')
Naming: IUPAC—Alkoxyalkanes (e.g., methoxyethane); Common—ethyl methyl ether.
Structure: Oxygen is sp3 hybrid, bent geometry (similar to H2O).
3.2. Methods of Preparation
  • Williamson Ether Synthesis: R–O⁻Na⁺ + R'–X → R–O–R' + NaX
    Note: 1° alkyl halides best, steric hindered conditions give elimination.
  • Dehydration of Alcohols (Intermolecular): 2R–OH —(conc H2SO4, 413K)→ R–O–R + H2O
  • By reaction of diazomethane with alcohols (for methyl ethers): ROH + CH2N2 → R–O–CH3 + N2
Williamson Ether Synthesis: Tips
  1. Best for asymmetrical ethers: use 1° alkyl halide to prevent E2 elimination.
  2. Aryl halides (Ar–X) not reactive in Williamson (except with phenoxide, not aryl halide).
  3. For aromatic ethers (e.g., anisole): C6H5O⁻Na⁺ + CH3I → C6H5OCH3 + NaI
3.3. Physical & Chemical Properties
  • Low boiling (no H-bonding with self); soluble in organic solvents; slightly soluble in water
  • Highly volatile, pleasant odour, extremely flammable
  • Higher density than hydrocarbons of similar mass
3.4. Chemical Reactions
A. Cleavage by Hydrogen Halides (HI, HBr, HCl)
Cleavage order: HI > HBr > HCl (I⁻ is best nucleophile)
  • R–O–R' + HX → R–X + R'–OH (excess HX) → R–X + R'–X + H2O
  • Aryl Ethers (Anisole): Cleavage at methyl group, not the aromatic ring C6H5–O–CH3 + HI → C6H5–OH + CH3I
Mechanism: SN1 for 3°, SN2 for 1° and methyl ethers
B. Formation of Peroxides
R–O–R' + O2 (slow, air, light) → R–O–O–R'
  • Peroxides are explosive, especially on storage
C. With PCl5 or SOCl2 (Chlorination)
R–O–R' + PCl5 → R–Cl + R'–Cl + POCl3
D. Electrophilic Substitution in Aromatic Ethers
Aryl ethers (anisole, etc.) are activated towards electrophilic substitution at ortho, para positions. Anisole + Br2 → 2-bromoanisole / 4-bromoanisole
  • NO reaction with NaOH (distinction from phenol)
3.5. Applications & Lab Importance
  • Common solvents (diethyl ether, petroleum ether)
  • Starting materials for plastics, drugs, perfumes
  • Former anesthetic (diethyl ether, obsolete for safety)

Trends & Miscellaneous Concepts
  • Acidity order: Water > Alcohol > Alkyne, Phenol > Water > Alcohol
  • Boiling point order: 1° Alcohol > 2° > 3° > Ether > Alkane (of similar mass)
  • Oxidation: 3° alcohols resist; 1° → acid (via aldehyde); 2° → ketone
  • Phenol does not react with NaHCO3; carboxylic acid does (CO2 effervescence test)
  • Ethers are less reactive due to resonance and poor leaving ability of alkoxide
