Part A — Alcohols & Phenols
🌿 Introduction, Classification & Structure

Alcohols and phenols are formed when a hydrogen atom in a hydrocarbon (aliphatic and aromatic respectively) is replaced by a hydroxyl group (–OH).

Classification of Alcohols

In alcohols, –OH is attached to sp3-hybridised carbon.

TypeFormulaExample
1° (Primary)R–CH2–OHC2H5OH (ethanol)
2° (Secondary)R–CH(OH)–R'(CH3)2CHOH
3° (Tertiary)R–C(OH)(R')(R'')(CH3)3COH
MonohydricOne –OH groupCH3OH (methanol)
DihydricTwo –OH groupsHOCH2CH2OH (glycol)
PolyhydricThree or more –OHHOCH2–CHOH–CH2OH (glycerol)
Allylicsp3 C, next to C=CCH2=CH–CH2OH
Benzylicsp3 C, next to ringC6H5–CH2OH
Classification of Phenols

In phenols, –OH is attached to sp2-hybridised carbon (benzene ring).

NameStructure
Phenol (monohydric)C6H5–OH
Catechol (o-dihydric)1,2-dihydroxybenzene
Resorcinol (m-dihydric)1,3-dihydroxybenzene
Quinol / Hydroquinone (p-dihydric)1,4-dihydroxybenzene
Structure of Alcohols
Oxygen in alcohols is sp3-hybridised → tetrahedral positions.
R–O–H Bond angle (C–O–H) = 108.9° (methanol) [Due to repulsion of two lone pairs on O] O–H bond length = 96 pm
Structure of Phenols
–OH attached to sp2 carbon; C–O bond acquires partial double bond character due to resonance.
C–O bond length in phenol = 136 pm Bond angle (C–O–H) = 109° (shorter than alcohol C–O = 142 pm)
IUPAC Nomenclature
  • Alcohols: Replace final 'e' of alkane with –ol. e.g. CH3OH → methanol; CH3CH2OH → ethanol; HOCH2–CHOH–CH2OH → propane-1,2,3-triol.
  • Phenols: Parent name = phenol; substituents numbered from OH carbon. e.g. 2-methylphenol (o-cresol); 2,4-dimethylcyclopentanol.
⚗️ Preparation of Alcohols
(i) From Alkenes
(a) Acid-catalysed hydration (Markownikoff):
C=C + H2O (H+) → C–C(OH)– CH3–CH=CH2 + H2O (H+) → CH3–CHOH–CH3 (2-propanol, Markownikoff product) Mechanism: Step I: Alkene + H3O+ → carbocation Step II: Nu attack by H2O on carbocation Step III: Deprotonation → alcohol
(b) Hydroboration–Oxidation (anti-Markownikoff, anti-addition):
6(R–CH=CH2) + 2BH3 → 2(R–CH2–CH2)3B (trialkylborane) OH⁻/H2O2 → 3 R–CH2–CH2–OH (primary alcohol) Syn-addition of reagent.
(ii) From Carbonyl Compounds
(a) Reduction of aldehydes and ketones:
R–CHO + H2 (Pd) → R–CH2–OH (1° alcohol) R–CO–R' + NaBH4 → R–CHOH–R' (2° alcohol)
(b) Reduction of carboxylic acids and esters:
RCOOH + LiAlH4 (then H2O) → R–CH2–OH RCOOR' + H2 (catalyst) → R–CH2–OH + R'OH Reduction with Na/alcohol = Bouveault-Blanc reduction
(iii) From Grignard's Reagents
C=O + R'–Mg–X → [C–OMgX] → C–OH + Mg(OH)X (dry ether) (adduct) (H2O) HCHO + R'MgX → R'–CH2–OH (1° alcohol) RCHO + R'MgX → R–CHOH–R' (2° alcohol) RCOR + R'MgX → R–C(OH)(R')–R (3° alcohol)
(iv–vii) Other Methods
(iv) Hydrolysis of alkyl halides:
R–X + KOH(aq) → R–OH + KX Use moist Ag2O (mild) to avoid elimination. Order: RI > RBr > RCl; t > s > p
(v) Hydrolysis of ethers:
R–O–R + H2O (H2SO4) → 2R–OH
(vi) From primary amines (nitrous acid):
RNH2 + HONO (NaNO2+HCl) → ROH + N2 + H2O (Note: methylamine → CH3OCH3 + CH3ONO, NOT CH3OH)
(vii) Alcoholic fermentation:
C12H22O11 + H2O (invertase) → C6H12O6 + C6H12O6 C6H12O6 (zymase) → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2↑ (sucrose → glucose + fructose → ethanol)
⚗️ Preparation of Phenols
(i) From Haloarenes (Dow Process)
C6H5Cl + NaOH (623K, 300atm) → C6H5ONa⁻ + HCl → C6H5OH (phenol)
(ii) From Benzene Sulphonic Acid
C6H5SO3H (oleum) → benzene sulphonic acid + NaOH then H+ → C6H5OH
(iii) From Diazonium Salts
C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + HCl (273K) → C6H5N2+Cl⁻ (warm with H2O) → C6H5OH + N2 + HCl
(iv) Cumene Process (Industrial)
Cumene (C6H5CH(CH3)2) + O2 → cumene hydroperoxide (C6H5C(CH3)2–O–O–H) + H+/H2O → C6H5OH + CH3COCH3 (phenol + acetone)
📊 Physical Properties of Alcohols & Phenols
Alcohols
  • Lower alcohols (C1–C4): colourless liquids; C5–C11: oily liquids; higher: waxy solids.
  • –OH groups form H-bonds with water → alcohols are miscible with water. Solubility decreases with increase in molecular mass.
  • BP higher than expected due to intermolecular H-bonding.
  • BP order: 1° > 2° > 3° (van der Waals forces decrease with branching).
Phenols
  • Colourless liquids or crystalline solids; become coloured due to slow oxidation with air.
  • Phenol is also called carbolic acid.
  • Form intermolecular H-bonds with both other phenol molecules and with water.
  • Higher melting and boiling points due to H-bonding.
⇄ Chemical Reactions Common to Alcohols & Phenols
(i) Reactions Involving Cleavage of O–H Bond
(a) Acidity — Reaction with Sodium:
2R–OH + 2Na → 2R–O⁻Na⁺ + H2↑ (sodium alkoxide) 2C6H5OH + 2Na → 2C6H5O⁻Na⁺ + H2↑ (sodium phenoxide)
Acidity order: RCOOH > H2CO3 > C6H5OH > H2O > R–OH Alcohols: 1° > 2° > 3° (more alkyl groups = more +I = less acidic) Phenol more acidic than alcohols: stabilisation of phenoxide ion by resonance. Effect on phenol acidity: EWG (–NO2, –Cl) at o/p → MORE acidic (stabilise phenoxide) EDG (–CH3, –OH) at o/p → LESS acidic (destabilise phenoxide) Increasing acidity: o-cresol < p-cresol < m-cresol < phenol < o-nitrophenol < 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid)
(b) Esterification:
R/Ar–OH + R'COOH (H+) ⇌ R/Ar–OCOR' + H2O R/Ar–OH + (R'CO)2O (H+) → R/Ar–OCOR' + R'COOH R/Ar–OH + R'COCl (Pyridine) → R/Ar–OCOR' + HCl
Conc. H2SO4 removes water → drives equilibrium forward.
Introduction of acetyl (CH3CO–) group = acetylation.
Aspirin synthesis:
Salicylic acid + (CH3CO)2O → acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) + CH3COOH
🧪 Chemical Reactions of Alcohols (C–O Bond Cleavage)
Reactivity order for C–O bond cleavage: Methyl < 1° < 2° < 3° (alkyl groups increase electron density on C–OH, easing bond cleavage)
(a) Reaction with Halogen Acids
R–OH + HX → R–X + H2O Reactivity of HX: HI > HBr > HCl Reactivity of alcohols: 3° > 2° > 1° Lucas reagent = Anhy. ZnCl2 + Conc. HCl → distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols (turbidity test)
(b) Reaction with Phosphorus Halides
ROH + PCl5 → RCl + POCl3 + HCl 3ROH + PBr3 → 3RBr + H3PO3 3ROH + PI3 → 3RI + H3PO3
(c) Reaction with Thionyl Chloride (Darzen)
ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2↑ + HCl↑ Both byproducts gaseous → pure RCl obtained.
(d) Dehydration
C2H5OH + Conc. H2SO4 (443K) → CH2=CH2 + H2O (CH3)3COH + 20% H2SO4 (358K) → CH2=C(CH3)2 + H2O Ease: 3° > 2° > 1° (Saytzeff rule → most substituted alkene major) Major product if cis-trans possible → trans isomer (major). Rate-determining step = carbocation formation. PCC (pyridinium chlorochromate): CH3CH=CHCH2OH → CH3CH=CHCHO (1° → aldehyde only)
(e) Oxidation Reactions
1° Alcohols: RCH2OH → [O] → RCHO → [O] → RCOOH (aldehyde then carboxylic acid) 2° Alcohols: RCHOH–R → [O] → R–CO–R (ketone) 3° Alcohols: resistant to oxidation; under vigorous [O] → cleavage Reagents: Alk. KMnO4, Acidic K2Cr2O7
Oxidation summary: CH3CH2OH → CH3CHO → CH3COOH CH3CHOH–CH3 → CH3COCH3 (CH3)3COH → CH3COCH3 + CO2 + H2O (vigorous)
(f) Dehydrogenation
R–CH2–OH (Cu/300°C) → RCHO + H2 (1° → aldehyde) R–CHOH–R (Cu/300°C) → R–CO–R + H2 (2° → ketone) (CH3)3COH (Cu/300°C) → CH2=C(CH3)2 + H2O (3° → alkene)
Lucas Test & Victor Meyer's Test (Distinguishing 1°, 2°, 3° Alcohols)
Lucas Test (Anhy. ZnCl₂ + Conc. HCl):
AlcoholObservation

2° (RCHOHR)Turbidity within ~5 minutes
3° (R3COH)Immediate turbidity/cloudiness

🔴 Chemical Reactions Specific to Phenols
(i) Electrophilic Substitution Reactions (–OH activates ring, ortho/para directing)
(a) Bromination:
C6H5OH + Br2 (excess) → 2,4,6-tribromophenol↓ + 3HBr (immediate white ppt — test for phenol) C6H5OH + Br2 (CS2 / CHCl3, 0°C) → o-bromophenol (minor) + p-bromophenol (major)
(b) Sulphonation:
C6H5OH + Conc. H2SO4 → o-hydroxybenzene sulphonic acid (minor) + p-hydroxybenzene sulphonic acid (major)
(c) Nitration:
C6H5OH + Conc. HNO3/Conc. H2SO4 → → 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) C6H5OH + Dil. HNO3 (room temperature) → o-nitrophenol (minor) + p-nitrophenol (major) ⚠️ o-nitrophenol is steam volatile (intramolecular H-bonding) p-nitrophenol is NOT steam volatile (intermolecular H-bonding → association)
(d) Reimer–Tiemann Reaction (formylation):
C6H5OH + CHCl3 (Aq. NaOH, 57°C) → [C6H4(OH)(CHO)]⁻Na⁺ then HCl → o-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) — main product + small amount of p-hydroxybenzaldehyde Electrophile = dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) [formed from CHCl3 + OH⁻] This is an electrophilic substitution reaction.
With CCl4 + Aq. NaOH → o- and p-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid as major).
(ii) Kolbe's Reaction (Carboxylation)
C6H5OH + NaOH → C6H5O⁻Na⁺ + CO2 (high pressure) → sodium salicylate → H+ → salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) Salicylic acid reactions: + CH3OH → methyl salicylate (iodex — oil of wintergreen) + CH3COCl or (CH3CO)2O → acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) + C6H5OH (esterification) → phenyl salicylate (salol)
(iii) Reaction with Zinc Dust
C6H5OH + Zn, Δ → C6H6 (benzene) + ZnO
(iv) Oxidation of Phenol
C6H5OH + Na2Cr2O7/H2SO4 → C6H4O2 (benzoquinone)
(v) Fries Rearrangement
C6H5–O–CO–CH3 (phenyl acetate) + AlCl3, Δ → o-hydroxyacetophenone (o-hydroxy acetophenone) + p-hydroxyacetophenone (major)
💧 Commercially Important Alcohols
Methanol (Wood Spirit)
  • BP = 337 K; colourless liquid.
  • Produced by destructive distillation of wood → "wood spirit".
  • Highly poisonous — even small amounts cause blindness; large quantities cause death.
  • Uses: solvent in paints/varnishes; manufacturing formaldehyde, dyes, medicines, perfumes.
Ethanol (Grain Alcohol)
  • BP = 351 K; colourless liquid.
  • Used in alcoholic beverages (produced by fermentation).
  • Solvent in paint industry; good industrial solvent.
  • Denatured by adding CH3OH (methanol) + pyridine + CuSO4 to make it unfit for drinking.
  • Used as CNS (Central Nervous System) depressant.
  • Burns cleanly → used as fuel (power alcohol when mixed with petrol).
Special Terms
  • Rectified spirit: 95.5% ethanol + 4.5% water; azeotrope; BP 74°C.
  • Absolute alcohol: 100% CH3CH2OH (no water); prepared by quick lime process or azeotropic method.
  • Methylated spirit: Rectified spirit + 4–5% CH3OH + pyridine + CuSO4 = denatured alcohol (poisonous, undrinkable).
  • Power alcohol: Alcohol mixed with petrol for use in internal combustion engines.

Part B — Ethers
💧 Introduction, Classification & Structure of Ethers

Ethers are organic compounds in which two alkyl or aryl groups are attached to a divalent oxygen (ethereal oxygen). General formula: R–O–R'.

Types of Ethers
TypeExample
Simple ether (symmetrical)CH3–O–CH3 (dimethyl ether); C2H5–O–C2H5 (diethyl ether)
Mixed ether (unsymmetrical)CH3–O–C2H5 (ethyl methyl ether); CH3–O–C3H7 (methyl n-propyl ether)
Aryl etherC6H5–O–CH3 (anisole); C6H5–O–C2H5 (phenetole)
Ethers are functional isomers of alcohols. They also exhibit chain isomerism and metamerism.
Structure & Nomenclature
Structure:
O in ether = sp3 hybridised → V-shape Dimethyl ether: C–O–C bond angle = 117.7°
IUPAC Nomenclature (Alkoxyalkanes):
  • Smaller alkyl group + O = alkoxy group (prefix)
  • Larger alkyl group = parent alkane
CH3–O–C4H9 → 1-methoxybutane C2H5–O–CH3 → methoxyethane C6H5–O–CH3 → methoxybenzene (anisole)
⚗️ Preparation of Ethers
(i) Dehydration of Alcohols
2C2H5OH + Conc. H2SO4 (excess, 413K) → C2H5–O–C2H5 + H2O (diethyl ether) Mechanism: I. C2H5OH + H+ → C2H5–OH2+ → C2H5+ (carbocation) II. C2H5–OH + C2H5+ → C2H5–OH2+–C2H5 III. → C2H5–O–C2H5 + H+
At 443K → dehydration to alkene. At 413K → ether formation.
(ii) Williamson's Synthesis
R–X + R'–O⁻Na⁺ → R–O–R' + NaX CH3CH2Br + CH3CH2ONa → CH3CH2–O–CH2CH3 + NaBr For phenyl ether: C6H5ONa + R–X → C6H5–O–R + NaX (alkoxybenzene)
Only primary alkyl halides give ethers (SN2).
Tertiary alkyl halides give alkenes (elimination — steric hindrance):
(CH3)3CBr + NaOCH3 → CH2=C(CH3)2 + NaBr + CH3OH
📊 Physical Properties of Ethers
  • Ethers are polar but insoluble in water (cannot form H-bonds with water due to absence of N–H or O–H).
  • BP is lower than alcohols of comparable molecular mass because ethers do not form H-bonds with water.
  • Dimethyl ether and ethyl methyl ether are both gases at room temperature.
  • Diethyl ether is a volatile, highly flammable liquid used as anaesthesia in surgery.
⇄ Chemical Reactions of Ethers
(i) Reaction with HX (Cleavage)
R–O–R + HX → R–X + R–OH (symmetric ether) R–O–R' + HX → R–X + R'–OH (SN2; smaller/less complex alkyl → halide) Reactivity order: HI > HBr > HCl If one group is tertiary: (CH3)3C–O–CH3 + HI → (CH3)3C–I (tert. halide, SN1) + CH3OH Aryl ethers: C6H5–O–R + HX → C6H5–OH (phenol) + RX (C–O of phenyl side NOT broken; ArO–C bond much stronger)
(ii) Halogenation
(a) C2H5–O–C2H5 + Cl2 (dark) → CH3CHCl–O–C2H5 (α-monochlorodiethyl ether, at α-carbon) (b) C2H5–O–C2H5 + 10Cl2 (excess, light) → CCl3–O–CCl3 + 10HCl (perchlorodiethyl ether)
(iii) Reaction with PCl₅
R–O–R + PCl5, Δ → 2RCl + POCl3
(iv) Reaction with CO
ROR + CO (BF3, 150°C, 500 atm) → RCOOR (ester formation)
(v) Electrophilic Substitution Reactions (Aryl Ethers)

In ethers, –OR group is ortho, para directing and activates the ring towards electrophilic substitution.

Bromination of anisole (methoxybenzene):
C6H5–OCH3 + Br2 (CS2) → o-bromoanisole (minor) + p-bromoanisole (major)
Nitration of anisole:
C6H5–OCH3 + Conc. HNO3/H2SO4 → o-nitroanisole (minor) + p-nitroanisole (major)
Friedel-Crafts acylation:
C6H5–OCH3 + CH3COCl (Anhy. AlCl3) → 2-methoxyacetophenone (minor) + 4-methoxyacetophenone (major)
Friedel-Crafts alkylation:
C6H5–OCH3 + CH3Cl (Anhy. AlCl3) → 2-methoxytoluene (minor) + 4-methoxytoluene (major)
💡 Uses of Ethers
  • Dimethyl ether: Refrigerant and solvent at low temperature.
  • Diethyl ether: Anaesthesia in surgery (general anaesthetic). Also used as a solvent for organic reactions (Grignard reagents).
  • Phenetole (ethyl phenyl ether, C6H5–O–C2H5): Solvent and intermediate in organic synthesis.
